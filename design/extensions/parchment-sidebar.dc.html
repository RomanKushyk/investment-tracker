T4 · The head: a capital band that carries the shell control, and no cluster

The ruling on the capital. Expanded, it is a 56 px band in the head, directly under the lockup, in the slot parchment-5h.dc.html:64 gives its ⌘K field and on that slot's own sb-field ground: a sb-label caption over the figure and its delta, bled to the shell's own edges. Collapsed, AppHeader carries it — which Layout.tsx:154 (showHeader = !desktop || collapsed) already arranges, so #108 changes that line not at all, no useCapitalCard caller moves and KpiCard tone="dark" stays where it is. The pinned cluster goes with the card, and the panel becomes the four bands the session drew: head, field, nav, foot. The rail is declined: 56 px of shell is 40 px of content, 149 016 ₴ is thirteen glyphs, and a segmented track at that width is the "third geometry" Two shells, one breakpoint rejects outright — the session's own rail draws no capital, no currency and no version either (parchment-5h.dc.html:376-381). The page is declined although the session draws the portfolio value as a content card beside the rail (:383-392): it makes the figure route-dependent, gone on /yield and gone on /settings, where the wall's promise is that it is always there — and it lands on a composition with no room, since screen-density.dc.html D-1 caps / at 944 = 560 + 24 + 360 and records that the row "keeps zero slack".

What the band keeps and what it drops. The card carried a caption, a 21 px figure and two numbers. THE CAPTION IS KEPT, and an earlier draw of this file dropped it — wrongly. Without it the band was a bare number under a wordmark: unlabelled on screen and unlabelled to a screen reader, so #107 would have owed it an aria-label that no drawing can show. The caption is the same t.sidebar.totalCapital the card used, at sb-label, and it is what makes the band worth its own plane rather than a stripe with a figure lying on it. The currency counter is the thing dropped: two lines hold the caption, the figure and one number, and the percentage is the reading the header bar puts first as well.

It is a band and not a box, and it carries no edge at all. It bleeds through the panel's 16 px padding to the shell's own edges, so it takes no radius either — a band that meets no corner has none to be concentric with, and the footer band's 29 is the one place that arithmetic bites. The honest reading is that the fill is the whole of it: sb-field on sb-bg is 1.12 in light and 1.07 in dark, so in light it reads as a band and in dark it barely does — and there is no fix inside the palette, because the best fill step the wall can take from ANY of its planes is 1.19, and only in one theme (card light, panel dark). The session's own .sb-field carries border:1px solid, and that edge is refused on the instruction that this is a strip; a field-border stroke (3.09 / 4.33) stays recorded as available rather than taken. What makes the band read is not its fill but its caption — a labelled two-line block holds together at 1.07 where a bare figure on a stripe does not, which is the whole reason the caption came back.

ONE GLYPH COLUMN, AND FINDING THAT THERE WERE THREE IS WHAT THIS BAND'S ALIGNMENT TURNED UP. Measured on the panel as it stood: the mark at 16, the capital at 16, the group headers, nav glyphs and dividers at 34, and the Settings glyph in the footer band at 30. The 34 is not arbitrary — it is the shell's 16, plus the 4 the Scroller viewport pays as its focus-ring allowance (Scroller.tsx:77-84), plus the pill's own 14 — and the footer band is 30 for exactly one reason: it is outside the Scroller and so never pays that 4. That is a defect, not a choice, and the drawing fixes it by giving the footer band 20 of padding where the nav effectively has 16 + 4, which puts every glyph in the panel on one line at 34 and every label at 60. The capital band's caption and figure join that column. What is left is a grid a reader can see: 16 the shell's own edge, where the mark sits; 20 the box edges, shared by the nav pills and the two tracks; 34 content; 60 labels.

The shell control loses its box AND its place in the lockup row, and ends up at the capital band's right edge. It was the panel's one absolutely-positioned element, it made the row reserve 38 px for a control 26 wide, and a bordered 26 px chip beside the DEMO chip read as a second badge. It is a bare ChevronLeft pushed to the band's end. The lockup row is what it should have been all along, three members and nothing floating: mark, wordmark, badge.

ITS SIZE IS SET BY ITS INK, NOT BY ITS BOX, and that is a thing this file had wrong until it was measured. A ChevronLeft paints six units of a 24-unit box across and twelve down, so in a 16 px box its ink is 4 × 8 — about a third of what sits beside it, while the box size says they match. Measured on the artboards: the rail's burger is 14 × 12, and the panel's nav glyphs run 12–13.33 wide by 10.67–13.33 tall in a 16 px box, median height 12. So the chevron is drawn in a 24 px box, where its ink is 6 × 12, and the height lands exactly on both. The width cannot follow and should not be made to: a chevron is 1:2 by construction and a burger is 7:6, so matching both axes would mean distorting one of them. Height is the axis the eye compares when two glyphs stand in one column, and height is what matches.

It sits in the one strip the panel reserves and never draws in, and the position is derived rather than picked. The nav column's right edge is 20 px short of the shell's own: 16 of shell padding plus the 4 the Scroller viewport holds back as a focus-ring allowance (Scroller.tsx:77-84). Nothing is ever painted in that 20, so what is centred in it is the glyph's ink — (20 − 6) / 2 = 7 a side. Measured as drawn: the pill's right edge at 223, the shell's padding edge at 243, the ink at 230–236, its centre at 233 and the strip's at 233. The 24 px box runs 221–245 and overhangs the padding edge by two, which the shell clips and which carries no paint: the box is scaffolding and the ink is the object, which is the same distinction the burger's 40 × 36 pressable region draws in T2. The arrow is in the panel's margin, not in one of its columns, which is why it collides with nothing and why nothing has to move for it.

WHAT THE BOX USED TO CARRY AND WHAT NOW CARRIES IT. The shipped control is size-[26px] rounded-[7px] border border-field-border (Sidebar.tsx:379-386), and its border was doing two jobs: identifying the control under 1.4.11, and being the target. Unboxed, the glyph is the whole of both. Identification: sb-item on sb-field reads 5.99 / 7.07, well over the 3 : 1 a non-text indicator is held to and better than the 3.09 / 4.33 the border it replaces read on the wall — T8 has the row. The target is the harder half: the drawn box is 24 × 24, which is exactly WCAG 2.5.8's AA floor with nothing to spare, and two of those pixels are clipped by the shell. The shipped comment argues that this control is ≥ md only, "where a pointer is the input, so it is one of the few controls TAP_44 has no business on" — that argument still stands and is why 24 is allowed, not why it is comfortable. #107 gives the pressable box 26 or more and lets the 24 px glyph sit inside it, which costs the drawing nothing because the box carries no paint.

Two placements are superseded, and both are worth keeping as the reason this one holds. The first draw put it in the LOCKUP ROW, boxed at 26, where it read as a second badge beside the DEMO chip and made the row reserve 38 px. The second put it at the band's LEADING position on the glyph column at 34, arguing proximity to the rail's burger — which bought 14 px of nearness to a control 25 below it and cost the band its reading, because a chevron immediately left of a caption says "collapse this band". The margin has neither problem: nothing else in the panel is a left-pointing glyph, and the sidebar leaves sideways and a group closes down does the rest.

One honest note, since the file measures both states of that strip: the arrow is outside the Scroller and the strip is defined by something inside it, so when the nav band scrolls the pill's edge moves in by the gutter and the strip widens 20 → 44 — measured in the 768 × 640 frame, where the strip's centre is 221 and the arrow's is still 233. The arrow does not re-centre, and that is deliberate: it stays 2 from the edge in both states, because a control that moved 12 px sideways when a list grew would be worse than one that is centred in only one of the two cases.

where-things-live.dc.html S5 settled the shell control against the group chevron by BOX — "boxed = the shell, bare = what it labels" — and that half is superseded; what settles it instead is the half the same section already calls free: the sidebar leaves sideways and a group closes down. One chevron points left and one points down, on one panel, and a box on top of that is a second cue for a distinction one cue already makes. Three bare chevrons on one panel and no box between them, which is what makes the axis carry the whole distinction.

One consequence worth naming before #108 meets it: the DEMO badge goes with the capital. It does not fit the rail (T7), and a dataset caution that disappears when a rail narrows is a caution nobody can rely on — so the header, which exists exactly while the rail is collapsed and below the breakpoint, does its second job.

And the decorative blob goes. Sidebar.tsx:271-277 draws a 200 px sb-field circle at 70 % clipped to the shell's 30 px corner. Composited it reads 1.09 against the wall in light and 1.04 in dark — decoration that identifies nothing, which is exactly the class Design pipeline exempts from 1.4.11 and exactly the class that has to justify itself on other grounds instead. It has none here: it sits under the footer band now, where the band's own fill covers most of it. #107 deletes SidebarDecor and its two call sites rather than redrawing it.

T5 · The two toggles, taken from the reissued session file

The ruling. The currency toggle and #85's theme control are one object at two widths, side by side on the footer band's first row, above Settings — the placement and the geometry of the session's own reissued file, which now draws a sidebar it did not draw before. A sb-seg-bg track with no edge, 22 px segments at flex:1 so each track takes half the row, and the selected segment as a solid accent chip. Three segments for the theme (Sun / Moon / Monitor, 14 px glyphs) and two for the currency — the two symbols and not the reissued file’s UAH / USD words, at 13 px, the size its own rail box sets them in. The theme control writes the stored theme preference — the same field the Settings Appearance card writes — and that is the one place the two are not alike: a currency is flipped to read one KPI and is gone by the next reload (Sidebar.tsx:311-315), while a theme has no glance reading, and system is never resolved at write time (Interaction rules, Rejected). A cycling icon button is declined: one glyph cannot show the two states it is not in, and system would be indistinguishable from whichever theme it resolves to. A Select is declined: it would be the only control on the wall that opens something.

THE BORDERS COME OFF, AND THAT IS WHY THE CHIP IS THE ACCENT AGAIN. The reissued file draws its track a 1 px edge and its selected segment a raised NEUTRAL chip on sb-seg-thumb; both edges were taken off on the owner's reading, and the neutral chip cannot survive without one. Measured, it identifies its state with a 1.36 / 1.25 fill step, where 1.4.11 binds a selected segment at 3 : 1; a 1 px field-border stroke would have carried it at 3.69 / 3.25, and with no stroke available there is nothing left in the neutral direction — the palette holds no fill that clears 3 against sb-seg-bg without being a hue. So the chip is accent, at 3.93 / 9.44, with accent-fg on it at 5.35 / 8.98.

HALF of Interaction rules' sidebar clause survives and half of it does not, and an earlier draft of this paragraph claimed all of it did. docs/DECISIONS.md:377-381 writes the exception in so many words — "whose currency toggle runs the wall's own field rank instead: a recessed track and a solid accent thumb" — and leans on it: "a solid fill therefore means a selected segment there and nothing else". The thumb half is restored by the borders coming off, above. The track half is falsified by the ground moving from sb-field to the active item's tint: the track is not the field rank any more. The leaned-on sentence still holds, because a solid accent fill is still the selected segment and nothing else. So the clause is rewritten rather than confirmed, and it does not travel alone: index.css:737-741 exempts "the rail's currency toggle, whose track is sb-field rather than a fill" from data-filled-track, and Sidebar.tsx:427-457 argues the same premise at length and prints the base ring's 5.05 / 9.44 on sb-field. All three rest on the same false sentence and all three are named in T10 for whichever of #85 / #107 lands second.

One repair survives, and it needs no colour the app does not have. The currency track's idle segment is TEXT — a currency symbol is a character and not an indicator, whatever its size — where sb-seg-text reads 3.61 in light against 1.4.3's 4.5, and the session's grid prints only its dark 5.16. So the currency's idle symbol reads sb-item (4.67 / 7.07) while the theme's idle glyphs keep sb-seg-text, which as a non-text indicator clears 3 : 1 at 3.61. That split is not an invention: it is the rank every nav row in the panel already uses, sb-item for a character and sb-icon for a glyph, applied to a row that happens to hold both.

Five names, and this file mints NONE of them. The reissued grid adds sb-seg-bg, sb-seg-border, sb-seg-thumb, sb-seg-text and sb-seg-text-active, and every one of their ten values is a hex the palette already holds — it re-values nothing and invents no colour. Two are refused because they hold ONE existing token's value in BOTH themes, which index.css:157-170 rules on outright ("two tokens that must hold the same value to be legible are one step drawn twice … do not re-mint") and #98 acted on for three edge names. Three are refused because the thing they named is not drawn: with the borders off there is no track edge, with the chip on the accent there is no neutral chip, and with the track carrying the active item's own tint there is no separate track ground either.

THE CHIP STILL SLIDES, and the drawing cannot show it. Sidebar.tsx:459-472 ships the selected segment as an absolutely-positioned accent thumb carrying data-owns-motion and a 300 ms ease-soft transform, and its own comment warns that a tinted track is what "would have flattened [the sliding chip] into the nav's own language". It does not, and the reason is that the chip is still the only solid accent fill on the plane — the tint is 12 %, the chip is 100. The artboards paint the fill on the selected <span> because a static sheet has no way to draw a transform, and that is a limitation of the medium rather than a ruling: #85 keeps the thumb, its motion and its data-owns-motion, and T10 says so.

THE TRACK CARRIES THE ACTIVE ROUTE'S OWN TINT, and it buys the file's worst fill step back. Against the footer band it reads 1.15 in light and 1.24 in dark, where sb-seg-bg read 1.06 and 1.02 — the figure this section had been recording as a cost for several drafts. The track is finally a shape rather than a rumour. What it costs is that the 12 % tint is no longer unique to the current route: three objects on the panel now carry it, one route and two tracks. That is survivable for exactly the reason Interaction rules gives when it writes the route's state — "a tint plus a 2 px inset left indicator, never a fill, so a state is never colour alone". The tint was never carrying the route on its own; the indicator was, and it is the only one of the three that has one. The other clause holds unchanged: a solid accent fill still means a selected segment and nothing else.

The costs, stated rather than discovered. One reading falls under its bar and it is the idle currency symbol: sb-item on the tinted track is 4.30 in light against 1.4.3's 4.5, where on sb-seg-bg it was 4.67. There is no rank between it and sb-item-hover, which reads 10.77 and IS the hover state, so taking it would collapse two states into one — the ladder has a hole in it, the shape floating-edges.dc.html T1 names. It is recorded at its value: one character, one theme, 0.20 under, beside reit's 2.79 and sb-label's 3.09. The theme track's idle GLYPH is unaffected, since 3.33 clears the 3 : 1 a non-text indicator is held to, and the accent chip goes 3.93 → 3.62 light and 9.44 → 7.75 dark, both still clear. And 22 is a drawn box, so below the breakpoint the row becomes two rows. 44 × 44 is hit area and never geometry (Two shells, one breakpoint), so each segment takes TAP_44's transparent overlay — at 22 drawn with a 1 px gap the overlays reach 11 past each edge and the collision is VERTICAL, not horizontal, and an earlier draft of this paragraph had the axis wrong. Segments abut sideways with a 1 px gap, so across it is WIDTH that decides: tap-target.ts's rule is w + gap ≥ 44, and measured as drawn the currency segments are 46.25 (clear, with room) while the theme segments are 30.5 and overlap a neighbour by 12.5. Down, the track is 22 tall and sits 8 above the Settings pill: 22 + 8 = 30 against 44, so those two overlays cross by 14 px — and that one is the real failure tap-target.ts names, an overlay reaching past its own control to hand the tap to its neighbour. Stacking the tracks one per row does not fix it, which is what makes it #85's problem rather than a note: a row gap of 22 or a taller drawn segment does, and both are choices the implementing branch has to make and measure. Nothing here forbids one row below the breakpoint — at 44 drawn, five segments plus the tracks' padding, gaps and a row gap come to 239 against the 280 px drawer's 248, so one row FITS, and the earlier draft's "one row is not available" was the opposite of what its own figures said. This is a requirement on #85 with its figures rather than a drawing, because the brief draws 1280 and 768 and the drawer's frames are mobile.dc.html's.

T11 · The literals that belong to no family

Forty-six distinct colour literals, forty-two of them token-backed and four confessed below.
