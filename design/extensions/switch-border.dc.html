The switch's boundary, re-derived

Mints --color-switch-border as a name — var(--color-field-border) in both themes, no hex, palette 45 → 46 — re-points dark's --shadow-thumb, and leaves --color-switch-track and the whole Scroller exactly where they are. Every figure is measured on the rendered app through the chrome-devtools MCP at 1440×900 and a true 360, dpr 1, transitions disabled and forced to finish, each theme reached by reload.

Supersedes the switch's OFF edge in the three boxes that draw one — asset-form :137 and :430, automation :365 — and the two prose specs that state the recipe. Neither file is edited. Ten boxes draw a switch across the two sheets; the other seven are ON and declare no edge at all. It also supersedes one clause of scroll-surface.dc.html:126's argument, and not one pixel of it.

Every switch in the app is drawn on card — the /settings Automation rows inside a Card, the AssetForm inside a Dialog, the quick-create inside its own bg-card box — so card is the plane it occupies and panel is the conservative one the repo's guards measure anyway. THE FILL IS NOT THE BOUNDARY, WHICH IS WHY THE REPORTED 1.03 IS NOT THE NUMBER THAT MOVES. #83 reports switch-track at 1.03 on panel. That reading is correct and it stands (T4-2): a compliant fill is arithmetically unaffordable. 3 : 1 on light panel needs L ≤ 0.24149 — a lighter fill would need L ≥ 2.573, which does not exist — and at that threshold the OFF↔ON separation falls from 12.19 : 1 to 4.20. In dark the threshold is L ≥ 0.15118; field-border clears it as a fill and takes OFF↔ON from 7.28 to 4.06 while flattening the fill/edge step to 1.00. The edge is the cheaper carrier and the correct one.

T2 · Why a name, and why not simply border-field-border

The value is not a choice — the palette already carries a control-boundary rank, and a switch's OFF edge is a control boundary. The name is the choice, and it is the mirror image of the question field-border.dc.html answered: there the two values differed in light, so "they must be free to diverge" was available. Here they are identical in both themes, so the case has to be made without it.

The counter-doctrine, conceded rather than dodged. index.css:160-166 retired --color-label into muted and ruled: "Two tokens that must hold one value to be legible are one step drawn twice. … Do not re-mint." The distinguisher is the word must. label and muted were forced onto one luminance by a single shared constraint — both were text on panel, so solving either solved the other and the second name could never move again. The switch edge and the field edge are held to the same bar by two independent measurements against two different fills, and they part the moment either control's fill moves. That is a claim about the future, and it is stated as a claim — so that the day one of them moves, this paragraph is the record of why the name was there to move.

T3 · The control, before and after, both themes

Real geometry, on the plane the control actually occupies: 40×22 at radius 6, a 1 px edge, 2 px padding and a 16 px knob at radius 4 sitting 3 px in. The two radii are proportional and derived independently — round(22 × .26) = 6 and round(16 × .26) = 4 — not concentric, and nothing here touches them. The ON state is drawn to show that it does not move: it carries border-ink on bg-ink, a 1 px geometric spacer that draws nothing, and it already reads 1.00 : 1 against its own fill.

T4 · The four rulings

1 · The name

--color-switch-border, declared var(--color-field-border) in both blocks. No hex, so no fourth grey; palette 45 → 46. The OFF edge points at it, replacing panel-border. It exists as a name because the switch's reason must live beside the switch's value, not inside a comment about fields (T2).

2 · The track does not move

switch-track keeps #e8e7e4 and #4a4a55. The fill is not the identifying boundary, and a compliant fill costs two thirds of the state cue in light (12.19 → 4.20) and the whole silhouette in dark (7.28 → 4.06, fill/edge 1.00). #83's 1.03 stands, with that arithmetic as its reason — and index.css:151-152 is replaced by it, not deleted.

3 · The knob's ring, dark only

Dark's --shadow-thumb re-points to the new name: 1.36 → 3.49. It stays a spread-only shadow, stays dark's one sanctioned shadow, keeps its single call site and still reads through the token. Light gets no ring — its knob is 1.24 on its own track, and that shortfall is recorded at the value, because the state is carried by the fill pair and the component by the edge. The ring is on the knob in BOTH states, so the ON halo moves too, 10.44 → 4.06 against the ink track: accepted, since it still clears the bar and that knob reads 14.16 against its track without any ring at all.

4 · The rail, re-affirmed

1.37 light / 1.36 dark on card, 1.20 and 1.53 on the sidebar plane — furniture that identifies nothing, and a rail that exists only while the region overflows. Nothing moves. One clause goes: "the rail already marks where the control is", because it makes the rail and the thumb vouch for each other. The exemption then rests on a scroll region is identified by its content alone, which covers both.

AN AFFORDANCE IS NOT AN IDENTIFICATION REQUIREMENT, and this file has to say so rather than leave it implied. Scroller.tsx:24-26 reads "The rail is the affordance, the thumb is the state" — sharper than the clause T4-4 deletes, so deleting only that clause would relocate the contradiction instead of removing it. WCAG 1.4.11 governs information required to identify a component or its state; a region identified by its content is identified whether or not its rail advertises that it scrolls. The same sentence stands unedited in mobile.dc.html:859-860, and index.css:671-672's (see Scroller.tsx) must still resolve to a standing exemption after the edit.

T5 · What this costs, and what it deliberately leaves alone

The cost is one palette name, one redundant declaration and one claim. The palette goes 45 → 46 with no new value, so the name is a synonym today and is defended as one (T2) — the claim being that these two boundaries are free to part, which is unfalsifiable until one of them moves. Unlike the four aliases it stands beside, all of which differ per theme, this one is identical in both blocks, so its dark declaration is a functional no-op kept purely to satisfy the one-for-one palette invariant: no precedent in the file has that shape, and a reader could mistake it for a second value. A third cost is the ON knob's halo, 10.44 → 4.06 (T4-3), which moves because the ring is unconditional. The second cost is honest and small: in dark the new edge sits 1.79 : 1 against its own track, and that is refused rather than fixed, because reaching 3 : 1 there needs L ≥ 0.31028 — nearest is muted #9b9a96, which is only 2.35 : 1 from ink and would make the OFF edge indistinguishable from the ON one. The field-border sheet's "or against its own fill" clause therefore does not transplant, and the settling fact is that the CHECKED state already reads 1.00 : 1 against its own fill in both themes and has never been called invisible. A switch is identified by its silhouette against the plane.

What it buys is everything the alternatives could not leave alone. switch-track keeps both values, so the 2026-09-01 ruling and its 7.28 : 1 state cue stand. panel-border keeps its value, so the rail, the two bg-panel regions, the table rules, the outlineMuted button, the two checkboxes, the import dropzone (#88) and light's toast-edge alias are untouched and need no re-measurement. faint keeps its value, so the Scroller thumb, the native scrollbar and every text-faint site are untouched — and the sub-3 : 1 rest that #80 declined to overturn is now argued for on ground that does not depend on the rail. Five shipped comments go stale and are named rather than left: index.css:418-419, :99-100, :95-96, :151-152 and Switch.tsx:7-8, plus Switch.tsx:41's "the OFF fix is the TRACK's alone", which this ruling makes false while leaving the ONE KNOB COLOUR ruling it sits in perfectly intact.
