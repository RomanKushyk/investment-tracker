T2 · Whether the bar binds a box two files call a container

This is the file's one real decision, and it is not arithmetic. Design pipeline holds a boundary to 3 : 1 only where it “identifies a component or its state”, and keeps one exemption open beside it: “a region's decorative edge and furniture that identifies nothing fall outside the bar”. The dropzone has a real claim on that exemption, and the claim has to be answered rather than waved past.

for the exemptionand why it does not carry
Both the drawing and the code call it a container: “the panel is a container, not a pressable — no lift, no scale” (data-portability.dc.html:436, ImportRow.tsx:112-115). Neither sentence is about identification. Both are about press affordance, and they are the reason it takes no lift and no scale — not a finding that it bounds nothing.
It is not focusable: no role, no tabIndex, no onClick, and it adds no second tab stop. 1.4.11 is not scoped to what the keyboard reaches. It binds what the pointer must find, and a drop is a pointer gesture aimed at a region.
The affordance is stated in words — “Drop a .json or .csv file here” — so the boundary is not the only thing that says so. The copy says what, and the boundary says where. Those are different facts and only one of them is written down. A drag carrying a file has to be released inside an extent the copy never states.
The operable control inside it, Choose file…, is border-ink and identified many times over. That is the keyboard path. It is the reason the box needs no second tab stop, and no reason at all for the pointer path to go unbounded.

Two tests settle it. Furniture that identifies nothing has no states — this box has three, and two of them are drawn as moves of the very border under discussion, which is a border doing identifying work by the drawing's own hand. And the one furniture exemption this app has granted, the Scroller rail's, rests on a sentence with no analogue here: a scroll region is identified by its content. An empty drop region has no content to be identified by. That is the whole of what it is.

Design pipeline also forecloses the shortcut this box invites, in terms: rejected is “exempting a boundary because its surface is not a field — 1.4.11 binds the component, not the class of token it happens to read”. Being a non-field is what the dropzone has been filed under four times. It is not a reason. The bar binds.

And the same test says two of its neighbours keep panel-border, which is the other half of this ruling rather than an unfinished job. Allocation.tsx:265 and TransactionPanel.tsx:707 are the app's only other bg-panel boxes carrying a panel-border stroke on a card, at the identical 1.44 / 1.40 T1 marks ✗. They are named here so the question is not asked a fifth time: both are static containers — no hover, no drag, no state at all — so the two tests above return the opposite answer, and they are what Design pipeline means by a region's decorative edge. The dropzone is not repaired because it is `panel` on `card`; it is repaired because it is the one of the three that a pointer has to find.

A hover that is weaker than the rest it replaces is the defect field-border.dc.html T4-3 refused muted over. Measured from a field-border rest, faint is that defect: it lies on the wrong side of the edge in both themes, so the box would go quieter under the pointer.

muted, because ink is spoken for. ink is the drag-over arm's own colour; taking it for hover leaves the two states sharing a border and distinguished only by fill, copy, weight and icon — a distinction removed rather than a stroke added, which is the cost #86 learned to name. And the reason fields need ink does not reach here: a field is focusable, so its hover competes with a 2px ink focus ring and has to be loud enough to be told apart from it. This box takes no focus. Three states stay strictly ordered on both adjacencies in both themes — inward, each on its own fill, 3.06 < 5.28 < 11.91 in light and 3.65 < 6.36 < 12.16 in dark — which is the property worth holding, not any one of the numbers.

Stated because it is the honest half: in light the hover step improves, 1.21 → 1.72; in dark it narrows, 2.18 → 1.74. Accepted, because a step is a comparison between two states and the reading against the surface is what a user has to see: the hovered edge goes 1.51 → 6.87 outward in dark, from invisible to unmistakable, and both states now clear the bar where neither did. A consequence that lands outside this file: field-border.dc.html:165 derives the fields' ink hover from a floor — “the weakest shipping hover, 1.55 light and 2.42 dark, both ImportRow.tsx:131” — whose only member was this dropzone. After this branch that floor has no member, and its figures were pre-parchment besides. The fields' ruling is untouched; the sentence that justified it is superseded, and field-border.test.ts carries a copy of it that #88 corrects.
