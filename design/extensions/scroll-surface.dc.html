Radii are D56's concentric half — outer = inner + gap, and the gap from thumb to rail's outer face is the 2 px padding plus the 2 px stroke. The proportional half cannot apply: it reads round(min(w,h) × 0.26) off two DESIGNED dimensions, and neither a rail's length nor a thumb's is chosen by anyone.

Both are kept because the reason they died is reusable: concentricity is invisible on a shape this thin. Nothing in the panel is near enough to a 12 px rail to compare arcs with, so the geometry buys nothing anyone can see — and the bar reads as detached from the edge it belongs to.

T2 · The gutter is the parent's padding

Why the same 28 px behaves differently depending on which box owns it

Wrong — padding on the scroller, scrolled right

padding held at scrollLeft 0 — and only there

Padding on the scrolling element is INSIDE the scroll box: it is scrollable content like anything else. At the start of the range it looks like a gutter; one drag later the text is under the rail on one side and against the panel edge on the other.

Shipped — padding on the box around it
та сама колонка, зрізана
за 28 від краю, на будь-якій
24 root + 4 viewport = 28, fixed

measured 28 at scroll positions 0, 400 and end. The 4 px on the viewport is the ring allowance — a scrollport clips ink overflow, and a focus ring is ink overflow, so a w-full control flush with the viewport loses its ring on both sides.

Three debts a scrollport creates
Percentage heights. Radix renders an auto-height display:table wrapper inside the viewport, so min-h-full resolves against nothing and mt-auto gets no free space. The sidebar's bottom cluster floated 16 px shy of the floor.
Focus rings. Clipped left and right on every full-width control. The old aside gave 16 px of clearance only because IT was the scroll box.
Keyboard scrolling. The panel used to answer PageDown; a Radix viewport is not focusable, so a dialog of non-interactive text became unreachable by keyboard (WCAG 2.1.1). It carries tabIndex={0}.

All three were found by review, after the first version shipped none of them. They are drawn here because a scrollport LOOKS like a neutral wrapper and is not.

Why a grid and not a flex column
The scrolling box needs a parent whose height is definite. A flex item sized by flex-1 under a max-h-clamped container is not: the percentage height quietly becomes auto, the body takes its content height, and the buttons paint over the fields. minmax(0, 1fr) is a real track — it has a size before its contents are laid out, and the 0 floor lets it shrink below them instead of being pushed open.
At 360 the split earns more than anywhere: the form is longest exactly where the viewport is shortest, and a Save button that scrolls away is one the reader has to hunt for.
