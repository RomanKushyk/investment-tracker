## D52 — A price cannot tell you both when it was struck and at what yield (2026-08-12)

**Decision:** the DCF model ships as a **staleness diagnostic first** and a
revision check second, because one observation cannot separate the two. The
verdict names the date confidently, names a revision only when it is too large
to be mistaken for staleness, and answers `inconclusive` where the price cannot
resolve the yield at all.

### The model holds, re-verified independently

`P(D) = Σ CFᵢ × (1 + y)^(−ACT_days/365)` over the published schedule, with
`returnRates.sell` as the only free parameter. Checked against the live feed on
2026-08-12 for UA4000238976 (y = 15.55%, quoted 1063.97):

| valuation date | derived | residual |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-10 | 1063.1303 | −0.8397 |
| 2026-08-11 | 1063.5513 | −0.4187 |
| **2026-08-12** | **1063.9726** | **+0.0026** |

Across the whole feed the same day: **19 bonds fresh, 4 stale by 4–6 days, 7
`completed` and therefore not applicable** — the seven matching D31's count
exactly, arrived at from today's data rather than carried over.

### The confound, which is the real finding

**One day back is worth −0.42 ₴. So is +0.08pp of yield.** The date search
therefore *absorbs* a small revision and reports it as staleness, and no
threshold can fix that — it is a property of having one number, not a bug.

A test pins this deliberately rather than hiding it behind a tolerance, so that
anyone later "fixing" the module into claiming otherwise has to delete an
explicit statement of why it cannot. The sharp check needs a **known** date,
which is exactly what the observation archive's `as_of` will supply (D50) — so
`impliedYield` takes a date and stays available for that.

One consequence corrected in code: when the verdict *is* `revised`, the implied
yield is solved at the date the quote was read, **not** at the best-fit date —
that date was chosen assuming the published yield still held, which the verdict
has just rejected. Using it would be circular.

### Yield sensitivity is a property of the bond, not a constant

Measured 2026-08-12, spanning two orders of magnitude and tracking time to
maturity exactly:

| ISIN | matures | ΔP per 0.05pp |
|---|---|---|
| UA4000235378 | 2026-08-19 (7 days) | **0.0090 ₴** |
| UA4000236624 | 2026-10-14 | 0.0795 ₴ |
| UA4000238976 | 2027-03-24 | 0.2658 ₴ |
| UA4000235782 | 2028-11-29 | **0.8842 ₴** |

A fixed residual threshold would therefore be wrong at both ends: crying
"revision" over pure rounding on a long bond, and claiming the yield confirmed
on one about to mature, where the price cannot resolve it at all. The noise
floor is computed per bond instead, and `inconclusive` is a real answer rather
than a fallback.

**This also corrects a claim made earlier the same day.** The three larger
residuals were first attributed to yield rounding; that holds for UA4000234413
(printed as `15`, so ±0.05pp is worth ±0.19 ₴) but **not** for UA4000235378,
whose 0.1968 ₴ residual is ~1.1pp at its sensitivity — far beyond rounding. Its
real explanation is that it matures in seven days, where the model is simply
not identifiable.

### Nothing computed is stored

Premises forever, conclusion never. The provider's stale value stays recorded as
the observed fact and the note only says so out loud — a `consistent` verdict
renders nothing at all, because a line on every healthy row is the noise that
stops anyone reading the one row that matters.

**Verified in the browser** on a live linked bond: UA4000234413 rendered
"Provider price is 6 days old — it still prices to 06.08.", matching the
independent measurement for that ISIN.
