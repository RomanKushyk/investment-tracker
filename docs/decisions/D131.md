## D131 — The mark is a Q with an arrow, and its old rules do not survive the swap (2026-09-02)

The owner supplied a new logo as five SVGs plus a token sheet. It is **a
different drawing, not a variant** of mark 04: an open `Q` — an r32 arc, stroke
13, round caps, gapped at the top-right — whose tail is a sand arrow, a 45°
shaft with a filled triangle head. The three path strings are byte-copies from
those files and were never re-keyed:

```
arc    M53 16.39A32 32 0 1 0 79.61 42.99    stroke-width 13, round cap
shaft  M52 44 74 22                         stroke-width 13, round cap
head   M79.78 31.78 64.22 16.22 86 10Z      fill + stroke-width 3, round join
```

The interesting part of this decision is not the drawing. It is that **three
rules in this repository were about the bars, not about the logo**, and a
straight swap would have left them standing over nothing.

### The even-centre rule dies, and the viewBox replaces it

`public/README.md` and `CLAUDE.md` both carried mark 04's pixel argument: bar
centres are EVEN (6/12/18/24) with an even stroke (4), because a bar spans
`[x-2, x+2]` and that halves to whole device pixels at 16px only when `x` is
even. It was a good rule and it is now meaningless — **an arc has no edge to
align.** Every tangent meets the pixel grid at a different angle, so there is no
parity to choose and no alignment to buy.

The only lever left on a curve is how much of the icon box the drawing gets, so
it gets all of it. The owner's files sit the paths in a 96-box with 9px of
padding via a generator transform (`translate(9,9) scale(1.0)
translate(-9.5,-8.5)`). That window is replaced by one cropped to the ink, which
drops the transform entirely and is exactly derivable:

- arc ink: centre (48,48), r32, stroke 13 → `[9.5, 86.5]` on both axes
- head ink: tip (86,10) with a 3-wide round join → x to `87.5`, y from `8.5`
- union: **78 × 78 at (9.5, 8.5)** → `viewBox="9.5 8.5 78 78"`

Confirmed in the browser rather than only on paper: summing each path's geometry
box and half its stroke width gives 77.9958 × 77.9976, the residue being
Chrome's own arc-flattening in `getBBox`.

That crop pays twice. A **36px** sidebar mark is now 36px of drawing — the
diameter the retired disc had, so the lockup did not shrink — and a **16px** tab
gets `13/78 × 16` = **2.67px** of stroke where the padded 96-box would give
2.17px. The 16px legibility the even-centre rule used to protect is protected by
geometry instead, just less precisely, and that is the honest trade: an arc at
16px is soft no matter what.

### What is pinned instead

`src/app/mark.test.ts` could not be adjusted, only rewritten: its `barCentres`
regex (`^M(\d+)`) and endpoint regex (`^M\d+ (\d+)v-(\d+)$`) assert non-null, so
the new paths would have **crashed** it rather than failed it.

What replaced those assertions is the drawing's own construction, asked of the
**parsed numbers**. It sits ON TOP OF the string equality, not in place of it,
and the division of labour is worth stating because the first draft of the test's
own header got it backwards. The string test is the stricter gate and it runs
first: the paths must be byte-identical in both copies, so a re-export that draws
the same curve with compact arc flags (`A32 32 0 10 79.61 42.99` — same curve,
legal SVG) fails there and never reaches the geometry. That is deliberate; the
two copies being ONE drawing is the property most worth keeping, and a re-export
is a thing to review rather than wave through. What the geometry adds is the case
equality cannot see — an edit applied to BOTH copies consistently, which passes
byte-for-byte and can still be geometric nonsense:

1. **The arc is a true circle.** Both endpoints sit r32 from the box centre
   (48,48), agreeing with the radius written into the arc's own `A32 32` — a
   pair a typo cannot satisfy twice. Measured 32.0030 and 32.0046; the
   assertion's slack is 0.05, and moving an endpoint one unit costs 0.375.
2. **The arrow is one 45° gesture.** The shaft's two ends, the head's tip and
   the midpoint of the head's base all satisfy `x + y = 96`. Exactly, not
   approximately.
3. **The head's base is square to the shaft.** Dot product 0. Exactly.

Plus one guard that is not geometry: the `Mark` block must contain **no `#`
hex**. "No ad-hoc hex in components" is the rule an SVG is most tempted to
break, because a designer's file arrives full of them, and the favicon — a
standalone file with no token to read — has to hold literals while the component
must not. Each new invariant was verified by breaking the drawing four ways and
watching the suite go red.

### The disc is retired — amending D56

D56 exempted four round things from its radius system: *"The **logo circle** and
the decorative blob stay circles. A circle among rounded rectangles reads as
deliberate — it is the mark, not another control."*

The new mark **is** a circle, so the 36px `rounded-full bg-sidebar-text` disc it
sat inside became a ring inside a ring. The disc is gone and the mark stands on
the lockup plate directly. D56's argument survives its own exemption: the round
thing among the rounded rectangles is still deliberate and still the mark — it is
simply **drawn now, not a radius**, so there is no `rounded-full` left to exempt.
Four live rules files named "the logo circle" among the app's round things and
all four drop it: `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md` §4, `navigation-map.md`, and
`src/README.md`, whose count was the sharpest of them — it read "`rounded-full`
survives in exactly four places … a fifth is a regression", and `src/` now holds
exactly three (`AssetAvatar`, `ColorDot`, the decorative blob). Nothing in the
suite catches that kind of drift: the claim ratchet counts claims per file, and
an unchanged sentence has an unchanged count. `docs/decisions/D56.md` and
`docs/design-briefs/phase-5/amendments.md` also name the circle and are
deliberately left alone — a decision is superseded, never rewritten, and a brief
is a record of what was drawn.

The mark keeps its 36px box, and that number is load-bearing for a reason worth
recording: the mark is the **tallest item in the lockup row**, so it and not the
text sets the plate's height — 36 + 20 of padding = 56px. Anything else would
move the DEMO badge and the collapse control, both pinned to that plate's
corner.

That last sentence only became true today, which is worth knowing before someone
"restores" the wordmark's size. At 16px the **text block** measured 36.78px and
set the height itself, for a 56.78px plate; at 14px it measures 32.19px and the
mark takes the height back, 0.78px shorter. So the two halves of this decision
are coupled: the wordmark's size is what decides which item is the taller, and
the plate height moves either way.

### One brand token, the same in both themes

The app had 41 palette tokens and not one brand or accent colour; it now has 42.
`--color-brand-sand` is declared **at the same value in both theme blocks**, on
the precedent of `--color-pos-on-dark`, whose own comment says it is
"deliberately UNCHANGED: it was always meant for a dark plane".

The reason is that the mark's only plane in this app is `bg-sidebar-inset`, and
that plate is **dark in both themes** — `#333338` light, `#1c1c20` dark. A
flipped pair would therefore put the owner's light-ground value on the *lighter*
of those two plates, which is the worst pairing available:

| | on `#333338` (light) | on `#1c1c20` (dark) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `#d8b494` — chosen | **6.51** | **8.80** |
| `#9c683a` — the owner's light value | 2.67 — fails WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 | 3.60 |

That second row is how a flipped token passes review in one theme and fails in
the other. Both chosen figures were confirmed against the colours the page
actually resolved, not only computed: the arrow is `rgb(216, 180, 148)` in both
themes, the arc `rgb(233, 232, 230)` light and `rgb(236, 234, 231)` dark.

`#9c683a` is not unused. It is the light branch of `public/favicon.svg`, whose
ground is the browser's tab strip and genuinely light — 4.71:1 on white. That
file carries **four** literals now rather than two, because the arrow is a second
colour, and it keeps its `prefers-color-scheme` block for the same reason it
always had one: the browser paints the tab, not the app.

### The wordmark — amending D54, and paying 2px for it

D54 assigned IBM Plex Sans to display and JetBrains Mono to body. The owner's
lockup outlines its wordmark in **JetBrains Mono ExtraBold at −2% tracking**, and
the sidebar wordmark follows it. This amends D54 **for the wordmark alone** — the
`INVEST TRACKER` tagline beside it keeps IBM Plex Sans, and now says so
explicitly with its own `font-display`, since the parent no longer supplies it.

It does not follow at the lockup's own size, and this is the change's one real
cost. Measured at 1280 × 900, demo dataset, desktop rail:

| | wordmark ink | clearance to the DEMO badge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IBM Plex Sans SemiBold 16px (before) | 73.5px (D66's figure) | 5.9px (D66's figure) |
| JetBrains Mono ExtraBold 16px, −2% | **83.53px** | **−4.93px** — it runs *under* the badge |
| JetBrains Mono ExtraBold 14px, −2% | **73.09px** | **5.5px** |

JetBrains Mono is a 0.6em-advance monospace, so the ink is `9 × 0.58 ×
font-size` and there is nothing to negotiate: the runway is 78.6px, fixed by the
244px rail, the 15px plate padding, the 36px mark, the 10px gap, and a badge
pinned at `right-[38px]` because the collapse control shares that corner (D66).
16px does not fit. 14px restores the clearance the D66 layout was tuned to.

The cost stated plainly: the owner's lockup sets wordmark:mark at 28/62 = 0.45
and the sidebar now runs 0.39. Matching 0.45 would mean a 31px mark, which would
take the plate to 51px and move the badge and the collapse control with it. The
proportion lost is smaller than the geometry that would have to move to keep it.

### Not done

The owner's five source SVGs are **not** checked in. `design/` is immutable
reference (D14) and holds the prototype drawings; the path strings above are the
provenance, byte-identical to the files they came from. The 222 × 62 lockups are
unused: their wordmark is outlined paths, and the sidebar keeps live, selectable,
translatable text.
