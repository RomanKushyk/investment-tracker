Append new entries to the bottom of this file. Numbers never change; code cites
them bare (`D61`). See [`README.md`](README.md) for the rules.

## D61 — Production sits behind Cloudflare's edge; the records that must not be proxied are named (2026-08-14)

D59 attached `quirenote.com` with every record DNS-only, because the only
requirement then was that ACM could validate. Production now serves real traffic
from a branch that is not redeployed daily, so the apex and `www` are **proxied**
and the rest stays DNS-only.

**What proxying buys, measured rather than assumed.** The immutable assets carry
`Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` (§1.3), and at the edge they
go `MISS → HIT → HIT` — verified by three requests through a Cloudflare IP. Repeat
traffic is therefore served by Cloudflare and never becomes Amplify egress, which
is the one cost line a flood could move. The HTML stays `DYNAMIC`, correctly: it
is `no-cache` by design so a deploy is visible immediately.

**Three records must never be proxied, for three different reasons.** The ACM
validation CNAME, because a proxied answer is Cloudflare's own address and the
certificate quietly stops renewing — the same trap the SES DKIM records hit in
A11. The mail records, because Cloudflare cannot proxy anything that is not HTTP.
And `dev`, because it is already closed by basic auth (D60) and a second hop buys
nothing.

> **Corrected the same day — `dev` IS proxied.** The reasoning above is kept
> because the mistake in it is the reusable part: it judged the record by what
> `dev` needs, and a DNS record is also something the whole zone publishes.
> Cloudflare's own warning made it visible — *"this record exposes the IP address
> used in the CNAME record on quirenote.com"*. `dev` pointed at the SAME CloudFront
> distribution as the apex, so leaving it grey advertised the origin the apex had
> just been proxied to hide, and the protection was worth nothing while its
> neighbour gave the answer away. All three hosts now resolve to Cloudflare
> addresses with no CNAME visible; `dev` still answers 401 without credentials and
> 200 with them, through the edge.
>
> **The other two stand.** The validation record and the mail records must remain
> DNS-only, and Cloudflare shows a similar warning on some of them: it is wrong
> there, and acting on it would break certificate renewal or mail.

**The SSL mode is the part that can take production down, and it was wrong.** The
zone was on `full` — TLS to the origin *without validating its certificate* — and
was raised to **`strict`**. Worth stating what the third option would have done:
on `flexible` Cloudflare speaks plain HTTP to an origin that answers
301-to-HTTPS, and the apex serves an infinite redirect. That is why the change was
made on `www` first as a canary and only then on the apex: a redirect loop on
`www` is a nuisance, on the apex it is an outage.

**Two certificates now, both checked.** Visitors get Cloudflare's Universal SSL
(`CN=quirenote.com`, Google Trust Services, auto-renewed); Cloudflare reaches
CloudFront over the ACM `*.quirenote.com` certificate that Amplify manages. The
Amplify domain association stayed `AVAILABLE` through the change — checked
afterwards, because Amplify re-verifies the domain and the apex no longer resolves
to its CloudFront target.

**This does not reverse D40.** DNS is still Cloudflare's rather than Route 53's,
still free, and still adds no standing AWS charge. What changed is that Cloudflare
now serves the bytes as well as the answers.

## D62 — SMS is not a way around the SES denial; it trades one queue for two at 1,615× the price (2026-08-14)

Investigated after the SES production-access request was denied (A11 audit, same
day), on the question of whether an SMS one-time code could replace the email the
request exists to send. Answer: no, and the reasons are numbers rather than taste.

**What SMS would have to replace is one message, not a channel.** D39 made
onboarding passkey-first precisely so that steady state sends nothing: the whole
life of an account is **two** messages — the invitation, and a password reset if
one is ever needed. So SMS is not competing with "email"; it is competing with a
single invitation.

### It does not escape the approval queue — it doubles it

Measured on this account, not assumed:

| Gate | State | Mechanism to clear it |
|---|---|---|
| SNS SMS sandbox | `GetSMSSandboxAccountStatus` → **`True`** — verified destination numbers only | a support case, the same class as the SES one |
| Text-message monthly spend | **$1**, `MaxLimit 1`, `Overridden false` | Service Quotas request (`sms-voice / TextMessageMonthlySpend`, `adjustable=True`) |

Both are AWS-side reviews. The SES denial is a queue problem, and SMS answers it
with two queues instead of one.

### The price is the part that settles it

AWS's own per-country CSV (linked from the End User Messaging pricing page):

| Destination | Price per message |
|---|---|
| **Ukraine** | **$0.16154** |
| Germany | $0.10719 |
| United Kingdom | $0.05 |
| Poland | $0.03222 |
| United States (10DLC) | ~$0.0042–0.0077 |

Against SES at **$0.10 per 1,000** — $0.0001 a message — an SMS to a Ukrainian
number costs **1,615×** as much. And at the account's current $1 cap that is
**six messages a month**, which is below the two-per-user-lifetime this design
needs the moment there is a third user.

### One point genuinely in SMS's favour

**Ukraine needs no sender-ID registration.** `describe-registration-type-definitions`
lists 75 types and none is `UA_*` — while `BY_`, `RU_`, `KZ_`, `TR_`, `GB_` and
twenty others do exist. So the registration burden that makes SMS painful in much
of the region does not apply here. This was checked because the opposite was
assumed, and the API said otherwise.

### The structural objection from D39 survives the change of channel

D39 rejected email OTP because it puts delivery on the critical path of *every new
session*. SMS keeps that flaw exactly and adds one email does not have: **an SMS
one-time code is a way to spend the account's money from outside it.** Anyone who
can trigger a code causes a $0.16 charge; seven of them exhaust the current cap
for the month, and a raised cap converts the same attack from a denial of service
into a bill. Email OTP cannot be turned into a cost attack this cheaply, and a
passkey has no delivery step to attack at all.

### Conclusion

SMS does not solve the problem that produced it. What actually removes the
blocker is what is already planned: passkeys, so no code is sent on the happy
path, and the SES sandbox, which is sufficient while the only user is the owner —
whose own address can simply be verified. Revisit SMS only as an optional
*recovery* factor once there are users whose email has failed them, and never as
the primary path while the price is what it is.

## D63 — The environment split stops at the user's data; the archive has none (2026-08-14)

Owner ruling, taking O20's recommendation as written.

**The database split happens at W7, not before.** Until the app talks to a
server there is nothing to split: its store is IndexedDB, the browser scopes it
per ORIGIN, and `quirenote.com` and `dev.quirenote.com` are different origins.
Dev data cannot reach production today by construction — more thoroughly than a
server split would give, since each browser profile is its own store as well.
Doing it early would mean maintaining two of everything for a backend with one
job and no consumers; doing it at W7 means the migration is designed for two
environments rather than retrofitted onto one.

**And when it happens, it splits the USER data only. There is one archive, one
capture, for every environment.** The archive is public reference data — the
prices Inzhur and NBU published on a given day. Those do not differ between a
development build and a production one, and a second copy would be a second
history that has to be kept honest for no benefit. A dev cluster's archive is
worthless by construction anyway: the production archive's value IS its history
(D27 — Inzhur publishes none, so a day not captured is lost permanently), and a
fresh cluster starts empty. What a dev cluster can legitimately rehearse is
schema and migrations, which is a narrower job than "a place where dev data
lives".

The practical shape at W7: environments get their own **user** rows and their own
credentials; both read the same archive; exactly one scheduled capture writes it.

**The cost that decided nothing.** Money was measured before the ruling and is
not the reason for it: DSQL is $9.50/M DPU against a 100,000 DPU account
allowance the workload uses ~0.3% of, storage 34.6 MiB against 1 GB free, and
Cost Explorer reports $0.00 billed for DSQL this month. A duplicated stack would
add exactly one visible line — CloudWatch alarms, 7 today against 10 free, so
four of the fourteen would cost $0.10 each. **$0.40 a month is not an argument
either way**, which is why the reasoning above is about history and surface area
rather than about the bill.


## D64 — The capture retries by firing again, not by waiting longer (2026-08-14)

Owner asked for a retry when a capture fails — a provider under maintenance being
the case in mind — at no more than one attempt every two hours, and at least six.

**It cannot be a RetryPolicy.** EventBridge Scheduler exposes exactly two knobs,
`MaximumRetryAttempts` and `MaximumEventAgeInSeconds`, and spaces the attempts by
its own backoff: *"retry attempts with exponential backoff continue until either
the maximum number of attempts is made or until the duration of the
MaximumEventAgeInSeconds is reached"* (API reference). Exponential backoff
clusters attempts minutes apart. A site under maintenance is still under
maintenance five minutes later, so that policy answers a different question — and
it stays configured, unchanged, because that different question (a delivery that
fails to reach the function at all) is real too.

**So the retry is the schedule.** `cron(0 1,3,5,7,9,11 * * ? *)` — one schedule
resource, six firings, two hours apart. "Exactly one automation" still holds:
this adds firings, not timers.

**The window's ceiling is the feed refresh, not midnight.** Inzhur settles prices
at ~13:00 Kyiv and `asOfFor` derives `as_of` from the clock as (Kyiv date − 1). A
capture after the refresh would read the CURRENT day's prices and stamp them with
yesterday's date — wrong data wearing a correct label, which is worse than a
missing day. NBU is immune, because its file is fetched by date. So 01:00→11:00
is the widest window available, six is how many two-hour attempts fit inside it,
and the last firing keeps a full interval of margin against an early refresh.

**Constant spacing, against the usual advice, on purpose.** The AWS guidance
reached for here — exponential backoff with jitter — addresses many clients
retrying a throttling service on a scale of seconds. This is one client, one
request a day, waiting out a maintenance window whose length has nothing to do
with how many times it has already asked. Jitter's remaining value (not arriving
at exactly 01:00:00 daily) is available as `FlexibleTimeWindow` if it is ever
wanted; it stays `OFF`.

**A firing after the first is a no-op, and the guard is where the subtlety is.**
`alreadySettled` runs BEFORE the fetch, so in the ordinary case the providers see
one request a day exactly as before. It keys on **`ok = true`**, never on a row
existing — a failed capture writes a row too, and keying on existence would make
six firings behave as one. That is D43's lesson, which cost ~1,200 mis-flagged
rows the first time. `not_published` counts as settled as well: NBU publishes
nothing at weekends, and without that every Saturday would spend six firings
re-asking for a file that will never exist.

**Two defects found while measuring the old design.**

1. **The third in-handler attempt could never run.** The loop slept 30 s then
   300 s, so attempt 3 began at t=350 s against a 300 s Lambda timeout: the
   function was killed mid-sleep and the attempt it was waiting for never
   happened. The second sleep is now 60 s — the in-handler attempts exist for a
   blip, and anything longer is the schedule's job — and the timeout is 600 s so
   that two sources retrying cannot reach the cliff at all. Billing is per
   millisecond used, so the higher ceiling costs nothing.
2. **A timeout wrote no row**, so a killed run left no trace beyond the Lambda
   error metric. Fixing (1) removes the reachable path to it.

**What it costs: nothing measurable.** 30 → 180 invocations a month against a
1M free tier; ~75 extra GB-s against 400,000; the guard's indexed lookup is a
minimum-size read, so ~6 DPU a month against a 100,000 allowance the workload
already uses 0.3% of; 180 scheduler invocations against 14M. The providers see no
additional traffic at all unless a capture actually failed.

## D65 — Scrolling is a drawn surface, and its gutter is the parent's padding, not the child's (2026-08-17)

Every box whose size is constrained while its children are not was scrolling with
the platform's bar. Inside a `rounded-3xl` dialog that is a square-cornered track
running the full height of a rounded panel — a straight edge cutting the corner it
sits in — and it takes layout width on Windows and none on macOS, so the same
screen reflows differently per platform. `src/components/ui/Scroller.tsx` replaces
it everywhere, over the Radix `ScrollArea` primitive, with a native
`::-webkit-scrollbar` fallback in `src/index.css` painted from the same tokens for
the page itself and for any Scroller before its JS runs.

**The construction is 12 px across — `2 + 2 + 4 + 2 + 2`.** Rail stroke, gap,
thumb, gap, rail stroke. Twice the owner's Figma reference (node `6325:14975`),
which is the difference between a hairline and a touch target. THE RAIL IS DRAWN:
a thumb alone says where you are but not that the region scrolls at all — the rail
is the affordance, the thumb is the state.

**Radii are D56's concentric half, not its proportional one — thumb 1, rail 5.**
`outer = inner + gap`, and the gap from thumb to rail's outer face is 2 px padding
plus 2 px stroke: `1 + 4 = 5`. The proportional rule cannot apply here because it
reads `round(min(w,h) × 0.26)` off two *designed* dimensions, and a rail's length
is its panel's height while a thumb's is the viewport-to-content ratio. Neither is
chosen; both change without anyone deciding.

**The rail's margin is 8, equal on all four sides, and the gutter is
`2m + 12` = 28 — everywhere.** The distance to the parent's edge and the distance
to the text are one number: a box with more air on one side than the other reads
as misplaced rather than inset. There is no "content gap" separate from the
margin, and no phone/desktop split.

**Three earlier answers are superseded, and the sequence is the lesson.** Against
a rounded parent the inset was first `R − r` — 19 in a dialog, the gap at which
the rail's r5 shares the corner's centre *exactly* — then `R − 2r` (14), a relaxed
version of the same idea, then 14 on a desktop with the arc's floor (8) on a
phone, gated by a media query. All three are gone. **Concentricity is not worth
maintaining on a shape this thin**: nothing in the panel is near enough to a rail
to compare arcs with, so the geometry that justified 14–19 px buys nothing anyone
can see, while a bar pushed that far off its own edge reads as floating in the
gutter rather than belonging to it. The exchange is real estate — a dialog spent
40 px a side and now spends 28 — and the machinery it deletes is a breakpoint,
two custom-property pairs and a media query.

**The arc still gets a guard; it just no longer sets the number.** The floor is
where the rail's corner lands on the 45° point of the parent's arc: solve
`d = R − √(R² − (R−d)²)` and it comes out as `R(1 − 1/√2)` — 7.03 at a 24, 4.69 at
a 16, both under 8. That is *why* one flat margin is safe on every surface in the
app today, and `Math.max(8, ceil(R(1 − 1/√2)))` keeps it safe on a rounder one:
past R 27 the floor overtakes 8 and takes over. (2 px was the very first answer
and it is wrong: that is what it takes for a bar not to *touch* a value, not what
it takes to read one beside it.)

**The gutter belongs to the PARENT's padding.** This is the part that cost three
attempts, and the rule is the lesson:

> Padding on the scrolling element is INSIDE the scroll box. It guarantees a gap
> only at the ends of the scroll range; at every other position the content slides
> straight under a rail that floats above it. Padding on the box *around* the
> scroller takes the strip out of the scroll box altogether.

So the gutter sits on the ScrollArea root, on **both** inline sides, and **fixed**
— both, because the side without a rail still has to hold text off the panel edge
and the caller cannot do that with its own padding; fixed, because gating it on
the rail's presence makes a panel jump between symmetric and lopsided the moment
its content grows past the fold. Bands *outside* a Scroller (a dialog's header and
footer) pad themselves to the same number so all three line up.

**A Scroller manages the axes it is given, and the other one is
`overflow: hidden`.** A field wider than its panel is not merely unreachable, it is
gone, with no bar to say anything was there. Any box holding content it did not
author takes `orientation="both"`; `type="auto"` mounts each rail only on real
overflow, so the second costs nothing until it is needed.

**Two floors, on both axes.** A grid or flex item's automatic minimum size is its
*content*, so a scrolling band needs `min-h-0` **and** `min-w-0` or it refuses to
shrink, grows past its panel, and is clipped instead of scrolled. Missing either
one produces the same silent symptom: content disappears and no rail appears.

**Three things a scrollport takes away, and each has to be given back.** All were
found by review, after the first version shipped none of them.

1. *Percentage heights inside it.* Radix renders a `display: table` wrapper between
   the viewport and your children, and its height is auto — so a `min-h-full`
   column resolves against nothing and `mt-auto` has no free space. The sidebar's
   bottom cluster floated 16 px shy of the floor until the viewport got
   `[&>div]:h-full`.
2. *Focus rings.* An outline is ink overflow and a scrollport clips it, so a
   `w-full` control flush with the viewport loses its ring on both sides — the old
   aside gave 16 px of clearance only because IT was the scroll box and had `p-4`.
   4 px of the gutter is now the viewport's padding (`RING`) and the rest is the
   root's, so the content still sits `2m + 12` from the parent and the ring fits.
3. *Keyboard scrolling.* The panel used to be the scroll box and answered PageDown;
   the viewport is not focusable by default, so a dialog of mostly non-interactive
   text became unreachable by keyboard (WCAG 2.1.1). The viewport carries
   `tabIndex={0}`.

**One more, and it is a Tailwind trap:** the thumb carried both `hover:bg-muted`
and a `bg-faint` bound to Radix's visible data-state. Equal specificity (0,2,0),
data-variant emitted later, and Radix marks the thumb visible the entire time a
rail is up — so the hover never applied and the 3:1 value this entry rests on was
never reached. The state class was redundant with the base one as well as harmful.
Note the second half of the trap: **Tailwind scans comments**, so writing the dead
class into the note that explains its removal regenerates the utility.

**A dialog is three bands and only the middle one scrolls** — `DialogHeader`,
`DialogBody`, `DialogFooter`, in a panel that is `grid-rows-[auto_minmax(0,1fr)_auto]`.
A title that slides away takes the answer to "what am I confirming" with it, and a
Save button that scrolls has to be hunted for, worse the longer the form. A grid
and not a flex column: the scrolling box needs a parent whose height is DEFINITE,
and a flex item sized by `flex-1` under a `max-h`-clamped container is not — the
percentage height quietly becomes `auto`, the body takes its content height, and
the buttons are painted over the fields. `AssetForm` spans all three bands with
`display: contents`, which keeps every field and the submit button in one `<form>`
while its own box leaves the layout.

**Where the drawing lives.** `design/extensions/scroll-surface.dc.html`, a NEW
reference — `mobile.dc.html` was merged, and D14 says a merged surface is
superseded rather than reworked in place. The brief section that carries it is
`docs/design-briefs/phase-6-mobile.md` § S7. The first pass of this work edited
the merged file directly; that was undone and the file restored to what it said
on the day it merged, including the scrollbar spec this decision replaces.

**`Select` keeps the native bar, on purpose.** `RadixSelect.Viewport` owns its own
scrolling — keyboard navigation scrolls the highlighted option into view through
it — and Radix does not support nesting a ScrollArea inside it. The styled
fallback gives it the same 12 px width, 4 px thumb and r5 track, without the rail.

## D66 — Two shells, one composition; and 44 px is hit area, never geometry

**2026-08-17.** Phase 6 / A17, from `docs/design-briefs/phase-6-mobile.md` and
`design/extensions/mobile.dc.html`.

**The app has exactly two layouts and one switch: `md`, 768 px.** Below it the
sidebar is an off-canvas drawer and a header bar carries the capital; at and
above it, today's layout, plus a control that collapses the rail and hands the
header the same job. The 136 px rail is retired along with every `max-sm:`
override that served it — with a drawer it has no job, and keeping it would mean
maintaining three geometries instead of two.

**768 and not the 640 the rail switched at**, because the chrome costs 244 px of
sidebar plus 72 px of `main` padding: content only reaches 360 px at a 676 px
viewport. `sm` leaves 324, `md` leaves 452, and 768 is iPad portrait, which then
lands on the desktop shell exactly.

**The breakpoint is expressed twice and must stay one number.** `max-md:` in the
markup, and `(min-width: 768px)` in `hooks/useIsDesktop.ts`, because a drawer
needs a focus trap and a focus trap has to know which shell is mounted. It is
read with `useSyncExternalStore`, not `useState` + `useEffect`: the effect form
renders once with a guess and corrects it, which for a whole shell is a visible
flash of the desktop rail on every phone load.

**THE DRAWER IS THE SIDEBAR.** One `SidebarPanel`, laid out two ways; `variant`
decides only the two items that belong to a shell rather than to the navigation —
the collapse control, and the Total capital card, which below the breakpoint IS
the header and would otherwise be two truths about one number. Both figures come
from one `useCapitalCard`, so `headlineKpis` is derived once.

**Both shells are three bands and only the middle one scrolls** — the `Dialog`
shape from D65, for a measured reason: the sidebar's content is 851 px tall in a
740 px viewport, so the old `mt-auto` cluster sat below the fold on a phone and
on any short desktop window. Pinned, the currency toggle is reachable without
scrolling at 740 px of viewport height and at 640.

**The drawer's box is a Radix `Dialog`, not a new primitive.** It already supplies
every behavioural item S1 lists — focus trapped, `Escape`, focus returned to the
trigger, background hidden from assistive tech, body scroll locked and its
position restored. Verified: 18 Tab stops, none escaping; scroll 600 → locked →
600.

**Hardware Back closes the drawer instead of leaving the route**, via one
synthetic history entry that keeps the URL and spreads react-router's own state so
its `idx`/`key` survive. **It is a handler, not an effect, and that is the whole
of it working:** written as an effect it pushed the marker TWICE per open in
development, because StrictMode runs an effect body, its cleanup, then the body
again — so the push ran twice and the pop once, and the stack grew a dead entry
each time. Measured, not reasoned: `history.state` still carried the marker after
Escape had closed the drawer. A route change deliberately does NOT spend the
marker — by then the router has pushed its own entry on top of it, and popping
would walk back to the route just left.

**44 × 44 IS HIT AREA, NOT GEOMETRY, and that is what protects D56.** Growing
controls to 44 would move every radius, because `r = round(min(w, h) × 0.26)` is
keyed to the short side: nav pill 9 → 11, `Button` md 10 → 11, the currency track
13 → 17. Five radii and one concentric chain, rewritten as a side effect of an
accessibility fix. `components/ui/tap-target.ts` is a transparent `::after`,
centred, 44 × 44, never smaller than the control it sits in. It needs SPACING
from the caller as much as the class itself — overlapping hit areas hand the tap
to the wrong control — so the sidebar nav opens its column gap to 8 below the
breakpoint: 36 drawn + 8 gap = 44, and the regions tile the column exactly.

**Exactly two controls are allowed to grow, both of the daily ritual:** the quote
input (36 → 44, r 11) and `Button` size `md` (40 → 44, r 11) — the size variant,
not two call sites. `header` (36) and `sm` (30) do not move; `header` is 36
precisely so it pairs with the 36 px Date field.

**Text fields stay at 36 for two independent reasons.** G-2's own table names
`Input / Date field` among the radii that would move, and every phone frame in the
reference draws them at 36 — but the second reason is the harder one: **an
`<input>` is a replaced element and renders no pseudo-element**, so the overlay
technique is not available on it at all (`getComputedStyle(input, '::after')
.content` is `none`). A wrapper that forwarded taps would still hit-test the
overlay rather than the glyph run, putting the caret in the wrong place.

**Fields take 16 px below the breakpoint in ONE unlayered rule**, not a class on
eighteen inputs. Under 16 px iOS Safari zooms the page on focus and does not zoom
back out, and the app's fields were 13 px and 11 px. Unlayered is the mechanism:
Tailwind puts utilities in `@layer utilities`, so a `@layer base` version would
lose to every `text-[13px]` and silently do nothing.

**Two things the browser found that no gate could.**

1. **The `Scroller`'s colours were wrong on an inverted plane** — a pre-existing
   D65 gap the drawer only made unavoidable, because a 640 px-tall phone always
   overflows this nav. Chosen against `card`, the same tokens read **10.98 : 1**
   and **7.08 : 1** on the sidebar's `#26262a` against the 1.37 and 2.12 they were
   picked for: the quietest furniture in the app drawn as a white stick down the
   primary mobile surface. `[data-dark-surface]` now takes the DARK theme's values
   for those three tokens. **No colour is minted** — they are the values Phase 5
   already measured for a dark plane, borrowed on the one plane that is dark in
   both themes, the same move `pos-on-dark` exists to make. Re-measured on
   `#26262a`: 1.20 / 2.90 / 5.29, all inside the range D65 already sanctions
   across themes. The block is a literal no-op in dark.
2. **The 244 px lockup cannot hold a fifth element in flow.** A `[plate][button]`
   row leaves line 1 with 68.4 px and `Quirenote` is 73.5 px at 16 px IBM Plex
   Sans, so the wordmark ran under the DEMO badge by 5.1 px. Both ornaments now
   float over a full-width plate — which is what the 768 sketch draws, and the
   sketch could draw it because it draws no badge. Clearance is 5.9 px, measured.

**Three places where the drawing's arithmetic did not hold, and the intent was
kept rather than the number.**

- **The date sheet is 328 wide, not 312.** 312 is `360 − 2×24` and `312 ÷ 7 =
  44.6` is where the ">44 px cells" note comes from — but the sheet also carries
  8 px of padding a side, so the real cell is 42.0 and the target is missed. At
  the app's standard overlay margin, `calc(100vw − 32px)` — the same one `Dialog`
  uses — 360 gives 328 and the pitch is 44.3.
- **Tap-to-pin is wired to three charts, not four.** Seasonality is excluded in
  the drawing for the right reason and the code confirms it: `makeIncomeLabel`
  draws both the actual and the expected amount on the bar. Allocation is what the
  count is off by — it declares no `<Tooltip>` at all.
- **The sticky action bar is present whenever there is something to save**, and it
  rides the VISUAL viewport: iOS does not shrink the layout viewport for a
  keyboard, so `bottom: 0` would put the two buttons underneath it. Portalled to
  the body, because `position: fixed` resolves against a transformed ancestor and
  the route wrapper carries one for 300 ms on every navigation.

**What the phase measured at the end.** Zero horizontal overflow on all ten
routes — at 360 × 740, 568 × 320, 767, 900 and 1280, in **both themes**: a hundred
measurements, every one `0`. The A10 sweep left five routes over: attributes 133,
settings 82, daily quotes 57, overview and payouts 5 each. No focusable field
under 16 px. Every pressable 44 × 44 except the seven text fields above and one
inline link. No D5-pinned figure moved.

### Two rules the review added, and the defect behind each

**THE OVERLAY IS FOR A CONTROL THAT HAS A DRAWN BOX. Everything else gets a real
one.** `TAP_44` exists because a fill or a border cannot be grown without moving
its radius — but a control with NEITHER has nothing to redraw, so padding it to
44 costs no pixel and the neighbours are pushed apart instead of overlapped. That

## D67 — Production moves on a version, not on a calendar (supersedes D59's cadence)

**2026-08-17.** Owner ruling. D59 stands in every other respect — two long-lived
branches, one workflow, the environment as the real gate — and only its cadence
clause is replaced.

**D59 said: "it moves only when `dev` is merged into it — target at most
weekly."** The rule now is:

> **`main` moves when a new stable version is cut — MAJOR, MINOR or PATCH — or on
> demand.** There is no minimum interval and no maximum.

**D59's REASON survives intact; only its instrument changes.** The point it makes
is "a production URL that changes on every commit is a development URL with a
nicer name", and that is still the rule being enforced — production still does not
move per commit. But a week is an arbitrary fence around that idea, and a version
is the honest one: a version bump is exactly the moment someone decided a change
is worth shipping, and `docs/reference/VERSIONING.md` already defines when each
part bumps. Tying promotion to it means the gate is a JUDGMENT that was already
made, rather than a date that happens to fall.

**What the calendar rule cost in practice, twice in three days.** v1.6.0 was cut
on 2026-08-17 with the previous promotion on 2026-08-14, so the calendar said
wait until the 21st — for a release that was finished, tagged and green. And in
the other direction it invites the opposite error: a week with nothing worth
shipping still reads as a promotion due. Neither reading is about the software.

**"On demand" is not a loophole, it is the second half of the rule.** A hotfix, a
copy correction, a rollback do not always want a version, and the owner asking is
a sufficient reason on a one-user project. It is written down so that a promotion
outside a release is a normal, recorded act rather than an exception someone has
to argue for.

**What does NOT change, and this is the load-bearing half:**

- promotion is still `dev` → `main` **by fast-forward only** — no merge commits,
  linear history, enforced by the `protect-long-lived-branches` ruleset with no
  bypass actors (D60);
- `main` is still never branched from and never pushed to directly; merging `dev`
  is the only way it moves;
- the annotated tag `vX.Y.Z` still sits on the `chore: bump version to X.Y.Z`
  commit, which reaches `main` by the same fast-forward;
- `dev` still deploys on every push, and `paths-ignore` still skips a push whose
  every file is Markdown or under `docs/`. **That one bites harder under this
  rule**: a release whose only change is documentation now deploys nothing, so it
  needs Actions → Deploy → Run workflow.

**The consequence worth stating plainly.** Production's cadence is now whatever
the release cadence is. If versions are cut carelessly, production churns — the
discipline D59 bought with a calendar is now bought by the versioning table
instead, and that table is the thing to keep honest.

## D69 — The provider's FX rate is not stored at all — supersedes D30's placement of it

**2026-08-17.** Closes the last open box of `PLAN-NOW.md` A2 by **not doing it**.
D30 ruled that "the provider's FX rate is one number per payload and belongs on
`price_capture`, not on `price_observation`". Half of that stands and half does
not: it certainly does not belong on the observation, and it does not belong on
the capture either, because it does not need to be stored anywhere.

**THE PREMISE WAS WRONG, AND D31 IS WHAT DISPROVES IT.** The plan's box reads
"it is one number per run — every entry in a payload converts at the same rate,
proven in D31". D31 measured the opposite, in the same sentence it is cited for:
the four funds converted at **44.7579** (NBU's rate for 2026-08-10) while all 31
bonds converted at **44.8305** (NBU's for 2026-08-11), inside one payload.
Re-measured 2026-08-17 on a stored payload from another day, the structure holds:

| group | implied rate | conversions |
|---|---|---|
| funds | **44.8086** | 5 |
| bonds | **44.8568** | 4 |

One fund also yields 44.8087. That is not a third rate — `buyUSD` is published to
six decimals, so a rate recovered by division jitters in the fourth. **The number
is not singular and its recovery is not exact**, and a single `NUMERIC` column
would have had to pick one of the two and present it as the payload's rate.

**THE DECIDING REASON IS THE PROJECT'S OWN RULE, NOT THE CARDINALITY.** `A6`'s
last box states it: *premises are captured forever, the conclusion never is.*
`buyUAH / buyUSD` is a conclusion. Its premises — both figures, for every entry —
are in `payload_gzip`, which D48 kept inline on `price_capture` and which the
archive keeps forever. **Nothing is lost by not storing this, and the division is
available on any row, on any day, for as long as the archive exists.** The box's
own justification — "currently unrecoverable once the payload ages out of anyone's
attention" — is about attention, not about data. Attention is not a storage
problem, and a derived column in an append-only archive is a poor cure for it: it
would be a number that is forever slightly wrong in the fourth decimal, forever
one of two, and forever unable to say which entries it applied to.

**What this does NOT change.** D30's other rulings stand untouched — `basis` is
`buy | sell | nav | fair`, currency is not part of the basis, no USD value is
stored per observation, `instrument_ref` is `isin` for bonds and `slug` for
funds. The reason the USD value is kept off the observation is exactly the reason
the rate is now kept off the capture: it is a serve-time artefact whose vintage
says nothing about the row it would sit on (D31).

**If a future task genuinely needs the rate in SQL**, the honest shape is a view
or a derivation at read time over the stored payload — not a column written at
capture time. Whoever revisits this should read D31 first and notice that the
funds and the bonds do not share a rate, because the plan's own summary of D31
did not.

## D70 — Capture checks are structural; the value check is retired — supersedes D28's frozen-feed half

**2026-08-18.** Owner ruling, taken the same afternoon W1 measured the check it
retires. **The capture asserts that it RAN and that the payload has the SHAPE it
should. It no longer asserts anything about whether the numbers moved.**

**The ruling, in the owner's terms.** A price may change or not change for
reasons that are none of this application's business — maintenance, a weekend, a
public holiday, a holiday moved to the Monday after. Alarming on that
manufactures work where there is no fault, and that is the most expensive lesson
this project has already paid for: *an alarm that pages for nothing gets muted*
(D44, D45, D47), and a muted alarm is worse than none.

**AND THE CHECK WAS MEASURABLY WORTHLESS, WHICH IS WHY THE RULING COSTS
NOTHING.** W1 (`infra/README.md`) found that an `inzhur` digest can never repeat
while the feed is alive: 24 of the 31 live bonds carry daily accrued interest in
the published dirty price — a near-constant +0.43 **every calendar day, weekends
included** — and `quotes_sha256` is one hash over all 36 entries, so a single
daily mover keeps it fresh by construction. `unchangedDays` was pinned at 1: a
flat line carrying no information, bought with a 60-row query per source per run.
For `nbu_fv` the weekend and the holiday are already answered by the 404 path,
exempted from the failure throw by name.

**Retired:** `StalePricesAlarm` and its metric filter, `UnchangedDaysMetricFilter`
and the `unchangedStreak` query behind it, `StreakCheckLivenessAlarm` (which
existed only to prove the streak check was running, so it goes with what it
watched), `STALE_AFTER_DAYS`, and the `trackStreak` option.

**D28's hashing is NOT retired, and the distinction matters.** `quotes_sha256`
is still computed over the price-bearing fields only and still stored on every
row. The premise stays captured forever; only the *conclusion drawn from it on a
schedule* goes — the same line D69 drew about the FX rate. Anyone who later
wants the streak can compute it from the stored hashes, which is how W1 read it
in the first place.

**What replaces it needs no threshold, and that is the point.** The Inzhur
capture now names the refs it expects to still be listed — `TRACKED_INZHUR_REFS`
— exactly as NBU already names `TRACKED_ISINS`. A ref going missing sets `error`,
which reaches `ErrorAlarm` through the handler's existing throw. Refs rather than
a count, because D30 fixed `isin` for bonds and `slug` for funds and both kinds
are in one feed.

**Measured before choosing that shape, rather than after:**

| | `inzhur` | `nbu_fv` |
|---|---|---|
| `skipped_refs` non-empty | **0 of 14 captures** | **6 133 of 6 636 rows** |
| `entry_count` | only ever GREW: 34 → 35 → 36 | drifts 184–186 |

Those two columns are why there is no single rule over both sources. NBU's
`skipped_refs` is overwhelmingly non-empty because the backfill correctly reports
bonds that did not exist yet in 2016 (D43) — alarming on it would page about the
past. And a floor under `entry_count` would be a threshold guessed from one week
of a number that has never gone down; the first delisting makes it wrong.

**So both numbers are PUBLISHED AND NEVER ALARMED** — `EntryCount` and
`SkippedRefs`, per source. That is the pattern `ObservationsWritten` already
uses: a number worth watching drift on a graph, with no threshold honest enough
to page on. They are emitted from the scheduled handler rather than from
`captureOne`, which is what let `trackStreak` be deleted outright: a 2 600-date
backfill can no longer scatter points across ten years of graph, because it never
reaches the line. Structural separation instead of a boolean someone can forget
to pass.

**Alarms after this: five, all structural.** `SilenceAlarm` (the job stopped),
`ErrorAlarm` (a fetch, parse, zero-entry or absent-tracked-ref failure threw),
`DlqAlarm` (retries exhausted), `BackupAgeAlarm` and `AlertChannelAlarm` (those
mechanisms still running). Not one of them reads a price.

**2026-08-18.** Found by W1, confirmed twice, and repaired the same day.

**`as_of` for `inzhur` is the Kyiv date of the RUN. For `nbu_fv` it stays the
Kyiv date minus one.** One function served both until now, and the premise it
rested on — *"the feed refreshes ~13:00, so the 01:00 run reads the price settled

**MEASURED, AND THEN MEASURED AGAIN A DIFFERENT WAY.** Inverting the DCF over the
published coupon schedule dates a quote from the schedule alone, independently of
anything the provider says about its own calendar:

| DCF says the price is for | value | our archive filed it as |
|---|---|---|
| 08-15 | 1065.2373 | **08-14** (1065.24) |
| 08-16 | 1065.6592 | **08-15** (1065.66) |
| 08-17 | 1066.0812 | **08-16** (1066.08) |
| **08-18** | **1066.5035** | **08-17** (1066.50) |

Four consecutive days, a one-day offset every time, residual **0.0035 ₴** —
inside the 0.0007–0.0046 band D31 recorded for a fresh, correctly-dated bond.
Independently: Inzhur answered that fund prices are flat **Saturday–Monday**,

broken every one of them. The fix is to split the meanings, not to move the
subtraction: `inzhurAsOf` and `nbuAsOf`, two names, because the conflation is

provider contradicting your data is evidence about your data.**

## D73 — A branch always; there is no diff small enough to commit straight onto `dev`

**2026-08-18.** Owner ruling. D59's two-branch topology and D67's promotion rule
are untouched — this fixes the half neither of them wrote down: **who may author
a commit on `dev` itself.**

> **Every change reaches `dev` on a `<type>/<kebab-title>` branch and arrives by
> squash-merge. A one-line comment fix, a ticked checkbox, a typo and a version
> bump included. The only commits authored on `dev` are those squash-merges.**

**What was actually happening, which is why this needed a ruling.** On
2026-08-18 six commits reached `dev` and three of them — `cc22133`, `c708c30`,
`753008c` — were written straight onto it, while `c770488` (A20), `cf7a5c9`
(A19) and `5169b6f` (A6) came through `infra/*` branches. Nothing distinguished
the two groups except a private estimate of size: documentation and one-file
fixes went direct, code went through a branch. That estimate was never agreed
with anyone and was never the same twice.

**The defect is the threshold itself, not where it sat.** "Small enough to skip
the branch" is a judgement made by whoever is holding the keyboard, at the
moment they are most invested in not being slowed down — so it drifts one
commit at a time and only ever in one direction. A rule with no threshold cannot
drift. That is the whole argument, and it is worth more than the ceremony it
costs: a branch and a squash-merge on a one-file change is perhaps twenty
seconds.

**What the branch buys even on a trivial diff.** It is the unit of revert — one
`git revert` of one squash commit takes back exactly one intention, where a
direct commit stream has to be picked apart by hand. It is the unit of review,
including self-review: a diff read once more before it is merged is read once
more than it would have been. And on protected branches it is the only place a
change can be rewritten at all — `dev` forbids force-push with no bypass (D60),
so a mistake noticed after a direct commit can only be fixed by a second commit
on the record.

**The version bump is deliberately not carved out.** It is the most tempting
exception — one line in `package.json`, always the same shape — and carving it
out would reintroduce exactly the judgement this entry removes. It rides a
`chore/vX.Y.Z` branch like anything else; the annotated tag is then cut on the
resulting `dev` commit, which is where it was always cut, so nothing downstream
changes.

**What this does NOT change.** No merge commits, ever — squash and rebase only
(D60). `main` still moves by fast-forward from `dev` on a version or on demand
(D67). Branch naming, conventional commits and the no-AI-attribution rule are
as they were. This entry adds no gate to the branch itself: no PR is required on
a one-user project, and `gh pr create` remains optional.

## D74 — The funds' `nav` history is archived and never shown; the read-time conversion is rejected permanently — closes O21

**2026-08-18.** Owner ruling, closing `PLAN-OPEN.md` O21. The provider's Excel

## D75 — A value that came from the user's own hand is marked; one that came from the archive is not — closes O22 by dissolving it

**2026-08-19.** Owner ruling, closing `PLAN-OPEN.md` O22. **The question O22 asked
turned out to be about a model that is being replaced**, so it dissolves rather
than resolving — the same way O12 dissolved into D33, and into the same decision.

### What O22 asked, and why it stopped applying

O22 asked whether a settings toggle could make **G5** opt-out: the app writing a
daily quote snapshot with no Save press. That question only has meaning while a
snapshot is a STORED entity that something must write. **After the B3 migration
it is not.** D33 already settled the shape:

```
value(a, D) = units(a, D) × coalesce(user_price(a, D), archive(a, D))
```

Portfolio value is computed at read time. Nothing is prefilled because nothing is
written, and **G5 is untouched — not bent, not excepted — because there is no
write for a toggle to authorise.**

### The two options the owner put, and where each landed

**"Remove the toggle and automate completely — no presses at all."** Already true
for a LINKED asset after W7, by construction. **And impossible for an unlinked
one at any time**, because there is no archive row to read: the source does not
exist. That is not a contract limit, it is an absence of data. The demo seed
makes it concrete — none of its four assets carries an `inzhur` link.

**"Keep the toggle, per asset, and store manual entries separately."** The
storage half **is** `user_price`, already decided by D33. The per-asset half is
the genuinely new idea, and the research retired it: **the data already knows the
answer.** Whether a value can come from the archive is decided by whether the
asset has a provider link, not by a flag someone sets — and where an override is
genuinely wanted on a linked asset, `coalesce` gives it at **asset × date**
granularity, which is finer than any toggle.

### The ruling this leaves, which is the part that needed deciding

> **A value the user entered by hand is marked as theirs. A value taken from the
> archive is not marked.**

`coalesce` is silent by design, so without this an archive value and a
hand-entered one render identically — and the user loses the ability to tell what
they observed from what was published for them.

### It INVERTS D20, and the inversion is the rule holding rather than breaking

D20 marks the **machine's** value in the draft store: a quote draft with no
origin is the user's own, and that is *"the fact G5 protects"* (`core/types.ts`).
This ruling marks the **user's** value on the read model. Both follow one rule —
**mark the exception, never the default** — and the exception changes sides
because the default does: in a draft the user is typing and the machine's fill is
the surprise; in the read model the archive supplies every day and the hand-made
row is the surprise.

A future reader finding both will suspect one of them is a bug. It is not, and
this paragraph is why.

### What the evidence gathered for O22 keeps buying, even though O22 dissolved

It stops the question coming back in a different shape, so it is recorded rather
than discarded:

- **G5 is cited 23 times across 18 files, one of them in `infra/`.** Six are
  structural rather than decorative — `state/draft.ts`'s origins map exists to
  make "never overwrite a user value" decidable after a reload, and
  `fetch-quotes.ts` says G5 *"IS THE WHOLE POINT of this module"*.
- **An auto-save would have bought exactly one press a day.** The ritual is Fetch
  then Save; the fetch is manual by a separate policy (D19), so auto-save removes
  one of two.
- **And the machine's input is measurably imperfect.** The 2026-08-19 01:00
  capture reported `consistent 18 · stale 3 · revised 2` with
  `quoteMaxStaleDays 6` — five of twenty-three instruments' published prices
  either did not correspond to that day or fit no yield in the window. That is
  the argument for a human seeing the number, and it survives the dissolution:
  under D33 the human still reads the row, they simply have nothing to press.

### What this does NOT decide

**How the mark is drawn.** A chip, a weight, a dot, a tooltip — that is a design
question and belongs in a brief, alongside the same question for the `stale` and
`revised` model notes it will sit beside. It must not be invented in an
implementation task.

## D76 — Every branch is code-reviewed before it is squash-merged into `dev` — amends D73

**2026-08-19.** Owner ruling. D73 made the branch mandatory; this makes the
review mandatory on it. **It amends D73 rather than standing beside it:** D73's
closing paragraph says *"this entry adds no gate to the branch itself"*, and
this adds exactly one. Read them together. Together they are one rule with two halves: **the branch
is the unit of review, and the review actually happens.**

> **Before a branch is squash-merged into `dev`, `/code-review` runs on it.
> Findings are addressed, or declined in writing with the reason, and only then
> does the merge happen. No branch is exempt — a one-line comment fix, a ticked
> checkbox and a `chore: bump version` commit included.**

**Who runs it and when.** Whoever wrote the branch, at the point they would
otherwise say "ready to merge" — not after the owner asks for the merge. There
is no merge request in this flow (D73: no PR on a one-user project); the owner
simply says "merge it", and that should be a go-ahead on already-reviewed work,
so the review can still change the branch cheaply.

**Where a declined finding is written down.** In the **body of the squash-merge
commit**, naming the finding and the reason. With no PR there is no review
thread, so the commit is the only place that is permanent, searchable
(`git log --grep`) and attached to the change it is about — and on a
linear-history protected branch it cannot later be quietly edited.

### Why this is not ceremony, on the evidence of the day it was written

**On 2026-08-19 a single day's work surfaced defects in THREE documents that had
been merged unreviewed over the preceding three weeks.** They were not shipped
that day — they were *found* that day, which is the stronger fact: the oldest of
them had been in `dev` since v1. Every one was mine.

- `PLAN-OPEN.md` O23 said an alternative basis would read *"roughly +38 %"* over
  *"53 days"*. Computed, it is **+34,5 % over 55 days** — and the correct figure
  had been sitting in `navigation-map.md` since v1 as the rejected alternative.
- `navigation-map.md` claimed `/overview` shows *"exactly ONE banner"* on the
  untouched seed — a behavioural claim, not a figure. It shows two whenever the
  frozen coupon date falls inside the lead window, which is what it did that
  morning.
- The same file carried **three amounts a hundred times too large** —
  `687,02` · `484,36` · `216,00` written as `68 702` · `48 436` · `21 600`,
  the decimal comma dropped so an NBSP read as a thousands separator. **These
  date to v1**, and D5#3 had them right the whole time: the defect was three
  weeks old and nothing had looked.
- The Phase 7 brief pinned `Button md` at 44 / r11, which is the **below-`md`**
  value only; it minted a second save-failure string beside one that already
  shipped; and it left three dictionary strings pointing at a home the same
  brief was removing.

None of those was caught by lint, typecheck or 679 tests, because **none of them
is a type error — they are claims.** They took THREE different accidents to
surface: a design session reading the brief cold, a deliberate walk of the
testing map, and — for the first — **someone recomputing a figure the document
asserted**. That third one is the closest thing to a review the day contained,
and it is the argument in miniature: the figure was wrong, nothing flagged it,
and it only fell out because a person went back and did the arithmetic. A review
gate is the cheap, repeatable version of all three.

### Docs are NOT exempt, and that is the point rather than an oversight

The instinct is to exempt documentation and review only code. In this repository
that is exactly backwards: **the documents carry measured figures, pinned
contracts and instructions that the code cannot check.** Every one of the
examples above is in Markdown — four of four. A review that skipped them would
have caught nothing at all.

*(The first draft of this paragraph said "four of the five", and there was no
fifth. It was caught by the review this very entry mandates, on the entry
itself — an unchecked count in Markdown, inside the decision requiring that
unchecked counts in Markdown get checked. Left on the record because it is
better evidence than the paragraph it corrects.)*

### The cost, stated plainly

A review on a one-file docs branch is a minute that feels wasted, and it will
feel wasted most of the time. That is the same trade D73 already took for the
branch itself, and it is taken for the same reason: **"this one is too small to
review" is a judgement made by whoever is at the keyboard, at the moment they
are most invested in not being slowed down.** A rule with no exception cannot
drift; a rule with one drifts to the size of whatever the author is holding.

### What this does NOT change

The branch topology and the squash-merge are **D59's**; the linear history and
the no-merge-commit rule are **D60's**; the branch-always rule is D73's. All are
untouched — this entry adds one step to the merge and changes nothing else. No PR is required — the review is a
step, not a gate someone else operates, on a one-user project. `pnpm lint`,
`pnpm typecheck` and `pnpm test` stay separately required: a review is not a
substitute for the gates, and the gates are not a substitute for a review.

## D77 — A merged reference wins the LAYOUT, not the class list; three divergences on `/transactions`, recorded

**2026-08-20.** `docs/archive/design-briefs/README.md` pins that once an
extension merges, "the **reference** wins visual disputes". A35 built
`design/extensions/where-things-live.dc.html` § S4 and diverged from its markup
in three places while rendering what it draws. This decision says that is
allowed, and pins the test.

**The test: the reference owns the RESULT, the code owns the mechanism.** A
divergence is legitimate when the rendered geometry is identical and the drawn
one cannot express the case; it is a violation when the geometry differs. A
static sheet has no intermediate widths, no viewport height and no second
language, so it cannot draw states that only exist in a browser. Where it is
silent, the code decides — and says so where it decided.

**The three, all on `/transactions`:**

1. **`flex-[1_1_360px]` + a cap, not the drawn `flex:0 1 360px`.** Beside the
   ledger the rendered width is the same 360, because `max-w-[360px]` freezes
   the item and flexbox hands its unused space to the ledger. The difference is
   the case the sheet cannot draw: a WRAPPED line. Grow-0 strands the form at
   360 on a line of its own; grow-1 fills it to the 560 the screen shipped with.

2. **`gap-x-6 gap-y-3.5`, not one `gap:24`.** Three numbers existed for the
   stacked gap — 14 shipped, 24 in the row, 12 in the sheet's own 360 mock. A
   two-axis gap keeps the drawn 24 between columns without moving a shipped
   mobile screen by 2 px, which is a change that needs its own reason.

3. **The ledger's height is the viewport's, not the drawn `max-height:328px`.**
   328 is the mock clipping itself to fit a sheet; the sheet's own F6 note says
   the route shows all 18. `calc(100dvh − 197px − var(--app-header-h))` is the
   derivation, and `--app-header-h` exists because collapsing the desktop rail
   both widens the container and adds a 57px bar.

**What this does NOT license.** A35's first draft replaced `flex-wrap` with a
`flex-col` → `flex-row` container query, which looked equivalent and was not: it
dropped the width cap, so between a 561 and a 943 container the form stretched
to the full width — a geometry the reference does not draw anywhere. That is the
violation this test names, and it was caught by review rather than by reading.
**"It renders the same" is a claim to be measured at every width, not asserted
at one.**

**Supersedes nothing.** It refines the pinned rule the same way D56 refined
"shape": the reference is a contract about what is seen, and a class list is one
way of meeting it.

## D78 — A window's opening position is valued the DAY BEFORE it opens; the extension's figures are off by one boundary day

**2026-08-21.** `design/extensions/period-and-analytics.dc.html` pins the
windowed flows with the opening position valued **on** `from` — S5 ④ writes it
literally as `−140 977,62 @27.04`. A39 and A40 value it at `dayBefore(from)`
instead, and every windowed figure on `/yield` and `/overview` differs from the
sheet's by that one day's market move.

**The code is right and the sheet is off by one.** `transactionsIn` includes
both ends, so a purchase or deposit dated on `from` is one of the window's own
FLOWS. Valuing the position on `from` counts that flow twice — once inside the
opening level, once again as a flow — and the window reports a gain that never
happened. Valuing the day before is the only boundary at which each transaction
is counted exactly once.

It also makes the full history reduce EXACTLY, which is the property both
screens' builders are designed around: the day before the portfolio's first
transaction has no snapshots, so the opening value is 0 and every windowed
expression collapses to its unwindowed twin. Under the sheet's boundary it does
not, because the first snapshot may fall on the start date.

**MEASURED, so the size of the disagreement is on the record.** On the seed at
`3 місяці` the totals are 140 977,62 on 27.04 and 140 940,62 on 26.04 — a
difference of **37,00 ₴**, and there are **zero flows on that boundary**. So the
two boundaries agree on every seed figure except by that day's price move:
XIRR +12,08 % against the sheet's +11,97 %, total return +4 133,74 against
+4 096,74, capital gain +2 746,36 against +2 709,36. The choice is invisible on
this data and decisive on data with a flow on the boundary, which is exactly the
kind of difference a seed cannot show and a decision has to carry.

**This is D77 working, not an exception to it.** The reference owns the RESULT;
the code owns the MECHANISM. A boundary is mechanism, and the sheet's own MERGE
STATUS already records that its derived arithmetic is working notes rather than
contract. What the sheet owns — the placement, the classifier, the copy — is
untouched.

**Do not "fix" the code back to the sheet's figures.** If a future reading
prefers the `from` boundary, it must also change `transactionsIn`'s inclusive
lower end, or accept the double count, and say which.


## D79

**A derived schedule ASKS the walkers; it never re-derives the grid.**
`scheduledCouponMonths` (A41, answering D-5) first walked its own month grid
from `nextCoupon`, stepping by `MONTHS_PER_PERIOD` and stopping when the step
passed `maturity`. It looked like the same grid `nextUnsettledCoupon` and
`couponsInGap` walk. It was not, and it disagreed with `rollNextCoupon` in two
places at once:

1. **At maturity it BROKE where the roll CLAMPS.** …6475 is 03.12.2026 + 6
   months = 03.06.2027 against a 27.05.2027 maturity, so the walk stopped and
   травень lost a real final coupon. `rollNextCoupon` returns
   `{kind:'rolled', nextCoupon: maturity}` — a short final coupon is a coupon.
   Real Inzhur bonds pay every 182 days and drift off the month grid, so this is
   the ordinary case and …8976 passed only by coincidence (25.08 + 6m lands
   exactly on its 25.02 maturity).
2. **It ended the schedule by the CALENDAR where the app ends it by
   SETTLEMENT.** The S5 confirm leaves `nextCoupon` on the final date forever,
   so a "today" cutoff either kept a phantom February bar or dropped a live
   серпень one, depending on the day it ran — and the day axis kept drawing
   what the month axis had just dropped, because `couponProjection` takes no
   date at all.

Both are now `rollNextCoupon`'s and `nextUnsettledCoupon`'s to answer. The rule
generalises past coupons: **when a question is "what does this schedule do
next", the answer already exists as a function, and a second implementation of
it is a second opinion.** Two readings of one schedule is the failure, not the
arithmetic in either.

It also removes a walk that could not terminate early: `maturity` is optional,
so a semiannual bond without one collects two distinct months forever and the
twelve-month exit never fires — the first cut ran all 500 `MAX_GRID_STEPS` on
every render. The walk is now bounded by the calendar, twelve steps.

publishes at a different, unguessable URL and **polling a known URL never
signals anything**; the current link has to be read from the offer page. And
`Last-Modified` is a real freshness signal: the REIT file returns
`Mon, 06 Jul 2026 14:08:47 GMT`, agreeing with the date in its own name.

**What this does not touch.** D74 stands entirely — the rows are still archived
as published, still `nav`, still read by no screen, and the read-time conversion
to `sell` is still rejected. This decides only how the bytes arrive.
