## D114 — Segmented controls are filled (2026-09-01)

The owner reported that the price-mode toggle "stopped animating". It had not.
Measured in a foreground browser, the thumb travels **712.6 → 743.2** across one
300ms transition, `currentTime` advancing 16.7 → 266.7, exactly one transition
running. There was motion, and nothing visible moving.

### What was actually wrong

`card` on `panel`, measured on the shipped controls:

| | light | dark |
|---|---|---|
| chip vs track | 1.20 | **1.09, and inverted** |

`card` is LIGHTER than `panel` in light (#ffffff on #eceae7) and DARKER in dark
(#1c1c1f on #232327). A chip painted `card` therefore reads as raised in one
theme and as a recess in the other.

**This was not a slip — it is what the reference draws.**
`design/extensions/appearance-language.dc.html` specifies track `#232327` with a
`#1c1c1f` thumb, so the app had implemented the drawing faithfully, and the
drawing is what was hard to see. [`D57`](D57.md) already names the inversion and
compensates with a 1px ring — but the ring is `panel-border`, the same value as
the track's own border, so the chip's only distinguishing edge matched the edge
behind it.

### The idiom

The track is the plane's FOREGROUND, the sliding chip its BACKGROUND.

```
track  bg-ink      dark #eceae7   light #26262a
chip   bg-card     dark #1c1c1f   light #ffffff
active label   text-ink    on the chip
idle label     text-page   on the track
```

14.16:1 in dark and 15.07:1 in light, and it inverts with the theme without a
second set of values. Owner's model was the Settings switch in its ON state,
which is this same relationship.

Seven controls carry `data-filled-track`: the price-mode Σ/1, the theme picker,
the language picker, the Settings currency picker, the dataset switch, the
seasonality axis, and the asset form's fund/bond segment.

### The rail is the one exception, and that is a ruling

It was filled first, in the rail's own tokens — `sidebar-text` track, `sidebar`
chip, 15.95:1 dark and 12.31:1 light — because the rail is dark in BOTH themes
and an `ink` track would be a near-black bar on a near-black rail in light.

Then the review found what the colours could not: **the selection read
backwards.** `pillClass` paints the ACTIVE route `bg-sidebar-text` with
`text-sidebar`. Filled, the UNSELECTED currency segment rendered exactly that —
twenty pixels under a list of routes where it means "you are here" — while the
selected segment took the rail's own fill, which is what an INACTIVE pill looks
like.

Owner's ruling, 2026-09-01: **the pills stay, the rail's toggle goes back.**
Track `sidebar-inset`, chip `sidebar-text`, active label `text-sidebar`, idle
`text-sidebar-nav`. In this plane a light lozenge means SELECTED, and a control
sitting inside it does not get to invert that.

It takes no `data-filled-track` with it: on a dark track the focus ring wants to
stay light, which `[data-dark-surface] :focus-visible` already gives it.

### A filled track needs the ring in the background

The base rule paints the ring `var(--color-ink)`, so on an `ink` track it was
**1.00:1 — invisible — on every segment of every filled control**, measured with
transitions disabled. (A first draft of this entry blamed `currentColor` and
said the idle segments were unaffected; both came from sampling an animated
property mid-flight. There is no `currentColor` outline in the repo.)

In the rail it reinstated the exact bug
`[data-dark-surface] :focus-visible` was written for: that rule's own comment
says keyboard focus had been "invisible on every nav pill and both toggles".

`[data-filled-track] :focus-visible` sets `page`, and shortens the offset to 1 so a 2px ring fits the tightest filled track's 3px inset. Opt-in by attribute, the shape
`[data-dark-surface]` already uses — a control on `panel` or `card` is served by
the base rule, and so is the rail, whose track stays dark. After: 13.84:1 light,
15.32:1 dark, on every filled segment.

**Measuring this needs transitions off.** Tailwind's `transition` utility
animates `outline-color`, so any sample inside 300ms of focus catches the ring
mid-flight; the same button read 1.00, 13.47 and 13.84 depending on when.

### What is retired

- **`--color-segment-thumb`** (#454550 in dark, a mid-grey chip at 1.66:1) — the
  intermediate step this replaces, minted and retired the same day, unused.
- **`--shadow-thumb` on the segmented thumbs.** Its justification was the
  inverting surface step; on an `ink` track a `card` chip is ~14:1 either way, and
  on the price-mode track — 2px of padding — a 1px outward spread filled half the
  gap it was meant to sit in. The Switch keeps it: its OFF knob is the one case
  still resting on a near neighbour.

### Also settled the same day

- **The Switch's OFF state**, which D57 measured at 1.19:1 and left. Its track
  goes to `#4a4a55` (`--color-switch-track`), taking the TRACK itself from 1.19:1 to
  1.94:1 against the card behind it — the part D57 did not name. The KNOB is
  unchanged at 1.94:1, and that is the same number for a plain reason: the knob
  IS `card`. An earlier draft recorded 8.74:1 here, which is white on that
  track — the reverted revision, not what ships. The knob stays `card`: owner's ruling after seeing a white one, since
  a knob that changes colour with the state makes one control look like two.
- **The price-mode toggle's footprint**, set to the Settings switch's 40 × 22.
  It costs WCAG 2.5.8 AA — segments are 15 × 16 where 26.6 × 24.5 cleared
  24 × 24, and `TAP_44` cannot help on a 19 pitch. Accepted knowingly, and the
  docblock records it as a regression — but NOT as a one-line revert, which an
  earlier draft of this line called it. Undoing the footprint is six edits: the
  track loses `w-10` as well as returning to `h-[32px] p-[3px]`, the segments
  regain `px-2.5 py-1`, and three radii are literals that do not follow — segment
  and thumb 4 → 6, track 7 → 10 — plus the thumb's own `-4px` and `[2px]`.

### What this supersedes

D57's reading that the segmented control's thumb is `card` on `panel`, and its
count of four `--shadow-thumb` call sites. D57's own subject — that zeroing the
dark shadows is what breaks a surface step — stands unchanged, and is the reason
this decision exists.
