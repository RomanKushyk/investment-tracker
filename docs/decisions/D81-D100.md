## D81

**Every insight card that summarises the bars reads the same ledger the bars
do; the one that describes a schedule does not.** A42, 2026-08-24.

The merged sheet's spine classifies `/seasonality`'s BARS — actual FLOW,
expected FORECAST — and stops there. The three cards beneath them are this
repo's to classify, and leaving it implicit produced a screen that disagreed
with itself inside one viewport.

- **«Якір доходу» WINDOWS.** It names a day derived from the windowed bars, so
  its growth figures must come from the same side of the boundary: at full
  history «580 ₴ → 700 ₴», under `3 місяці` «472 ₴ → 700 ₴». Unwindowed it put
  February's dividend in one breath with a day derived from May onward.
- **«Тиха смуга» WINDOWS**, for the same reason and with a caveat worth writing
  down: a narrow window manufactures the quiet it reports. That is accepted —
  a card that summarises the bars must agree with them — and it is why the
  claim is phrased as an observation rather than a rule.
- **«Купонний сезон» IS HALF-HISTORICAL, and that half windows.** Calling it a
  schedule and leaving it whole was too clean: `bondCouponInfo`'s months are the
  months a bond HAS PAID in, union the one `nextCoupon` names. Only the second
  is a forecast. Unwindowed, the card headlined «лютий і серпень» while the
  chart drew no лютий bar, because лютий came from a payout the same screen had
  just excluded.

**The rule generalises past this screen:** a derived statement inherits the
classification of the data it is derived FROM, not the classification of the
sentence it is written as. A card about "seasons" sounds like a forecast and is
mostly a sum.

**And the attribution follows the same rule.** A bar's colour comes from
`dominantAssetOnDay`, which is a FLOW question — which asset paid most on this
day-of-month — so a windowed height may not wear an unwindowed hue.

*(Recorded because the sheet does not cover it and the next person to change a
card needs something numbered to supersede.)*

## D82

**SMIDA's open-data API is alive and always was — D27's "retired 2021-06-30
(verified: 404)" recorded the wrong host.** Measured 2026-08-24.

D27 and `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-04-data-model.md` (line 241) both carry
the sentence *"НКЦПФР's open datasets contain no NAV; SMIDA's open-data API was
retired 2021-06-30 (verified: 404)"*. The first half is unchanged. **The second
half is wrong**, and the mistake is identifiable rather than merely corrected:

minutes before the read, and the count advanced by four during the half hour of
measurement. It is published under **art. 10¹ of the Law on Access to Public
Information** with a documented query grammar (`limit`, `idlast`, `edrpou`,
`period`, `date`, `sdate`, `fdate`), and it is reachable from a non-Ukrainian

**What was actually retired on 2021-06-30 is `stockmarket.gov.ua`.** Its five

database is SMIDA — which is how one could stand in for the other in a note.

**D27's RULING is untouched.** Inzhur's daily quote is still voluntary commercial
disclosure and still not a NAV; that conclusion rested on the disclosure regime

agency's feed was alive. What changes is only the availability sentence — and it
changes in the direction that matters: there *is* an official machine-readable
channel, it just carries filings rather than prices.

**Corrected where it INSTRUCTS rather than records, following D72's split.**
D27 keeps its wording — it is the record. The data-model spec is planned from, so
its line 241 gets a pointer to this entry. Note the two are not verbatim: D27
says "(verified: 404)" while the spec says "НКЦПФР's **19** open datasets" and
"(verified: **all paths** 404)".

**Two things this does not settle.** Whether we may fetch it is a separate,
still-open question — `smida.gov.ua/robots.txt` is a blanket `Disallow: /` with
no carve-out for `/db/api/`, which is `PLAN-OPEN.md` O25. And the same paragraph

## D83

**The provider's price files may be fetched automatically, superseding D72's
by-hand requirement.** Owner's ruling 2026-08-24: the less data that has to be
entered by hand, the better.

D72 ruled the `.xlsx` files must be fetched **by hand**, reasoning that
`robots.txt` disallows `/documents`, which is where they live, and that crawling
it while claiming to respect their crawl rules would be self-refuting. **The
premise was incomplete, not wrong.** Measured 2026-08-24:

- The files are also linked from the **offer pages** (`/offer/inzhur-reit`,
  `/offer/inzhur-energy`), which `robots.txt` explicitly allows.
- They are served from `d2zk2gr3fhkmim.cloudfront.net`, a **separate origin that
  publishes no `robots.txt` at all** — the request returns S3's `404 NoSuchKey`.
  **This adds no permission and is recorded so nobody re-checks it.** Silence
  leaves the question open, exactly as
  `docs/reference/MARKET-DATA-SOURCES.md` rule 6 says and as §4 there applies to
  `pfts.org.ua`. What carries this decision is the owner's ruling plus the
  allowed offer page — not the CDN's silence.
- `/documents` remains disallowed **and is not the path used**. The reasoning
  that made an automated fetch self-refuting therefore does not apply to this
  route.

So the ruling changes and the reasoning that produced it does not: we still do
not crawl what they asked us not to crawl. `/documents` and `/terms` stay
off-limits.

**Two mechanics the implementation must respect.** The filename carries a
**content hash** — `Inzhur_REIT_czina_06_07_2026_346a256fc9.xlsx` — so a new cut
publishes at a different, unguessable URL and **polling a known URL never
signals anything**; the current link has to be read from the offer page. And
`Last-Modified` is a real freshness signal: the REIT file returns
`Mon, 06 Jul 2026 14:08:47 GMT`, agreeing with the date in its own name.

**What this does not touch.** D74 stands entirely — the rows are still archived
as published, still `nav`, still read by no screen, and the read-time conversion
to `sell` is still rejected. This decides only how the bytes arrive.

*(Mechanics in `docs/reference/INZHUR-FUND-HISTORY.md`; W15's row in
`PLAN-WAITING.md` carries the scope this adds.)*

## D84

**Prettier is SCOPED, not retired — and the reason it failed 245 files was
never style.** A37, 2026-08-24.

The plan offered two answers: wire `format:check` into the gate, or delete the
scripts, on the ground that "a documented command that rewrites a quarter of the
tree is worse than no command". Both were premature, because the 245 figure was
mostly an artifact:

- **`endOfLine` was unset.** Prettier writes LF; this repo is developed on
  Windows with `core.autocrlf=true`, so the working tree is CRLF and every file
  differed on line endings before a single brace was considered. Setting
  `endOfLine: "auto"` dropped 245 to 206 — and, more to the point, means a
  Windows checkout can pass at all. It never could.
- **`printWidth` was unset**, so prettier used its default 80 against a codebase
  whose p99 line is 104. It was breaking `import { a, b, c } from './x'` at 93
  characters. Measured across widths: 80 → 135 src files fail, **100 → 98**,
  110 → 107. **100 is chosen on that churn count, not on the p99** — a width of
  100 is by construction BELOW a p99 of 104, so ~300 lines did have to break;
  the p99 says only that 80 was never a candidate.

**WHAT IT MAY NOT TOUCH IS THE OTHER HALF, and that is a rule and not a
preference.** `design/` is D14-immutable: prettier expands
`period-and-analytics.dc.html` from 2 729 lines to **7 535** — 5 979 added and
1 173 deleted, a 7 152-line rewrite of a file the repo has promised not to edit
and which decisions cite by line. (An earlier draft of this entry quoted 9 057
and 11 786; those were measured at the default width 80, before this same commit
set 100, and the second was a whole-file assumption rather than a diff. The
conclusion does not move, but a figure in this repo is measured.)
`docs/` and root Markdown are excluded for a different reason — prettier re-pads
every table cell to align its pipes, so a one-word edit to one row rewrites the
whole block (297 lines on `PLAN-NOW.md` alone), and it rewrites `*emphasis*` to
`_emphasis_`. On files whose value IS the text, that trade is backwards.

**RETIRING WAS THE WORSE OPTION FOR A REASON THE PLAN DID NOT NAME:**
`eslint-config-prettier` is installed, which switches OFF eslint's stylistic
rules on the understanding that prettier owns them. Deleting the scripts would
have left formatting enforced by nothing at all.

So: config fixed, scope declared in a `.prettierignore` that explains every
line, **110 files reformatted** in one commit — 104 under `src/`, three under
`infra/`, plus `eslint.config.js`, `index.html` and one script — and `pnpm format:check` added to the
CI quality gate ahead of `pnpm test` — before any AWS credential exists, so a
formatting failure cannot deploy. The reformat is verifiable as layout-only:
760 tests, typecheck, lint and build all unchanged across it.

**The cost is stated rather than hidden:** `git blame` on 110 files now points
at this commit. That is the price of the gate, paid once.

**Two things this entry got wrong before review, recorded because the repo's
rule is that its figures are measured:**

- **`prettier-plugin-tailwindcss` needs `tailwindStylesheet`.** Without it the
  plugin sorts against stock Tailwind, so every project token — `text-muted`,
  `ease-soft`, `bg-page` — is unresolvable and hoisted to the FRONT of the class
  list. The first cut therefore reordered classes in 17 files the wrong way and
  would have churned 36 more the day anyone set the option. Class order does not
  affect the cascade (CSS source order does) and nothing here imports
  `tailwind-merge`, so nothing rendered differently — but "layout-only" and
  "paid once" were both false until `tailwindStylesheet: './src/index.css'`
  made the sort authoritative.
- **`endOfLine: "auto"` is the shallow fix and is knowingly chosen.** The deep
  one is `.gitattributes` — `* text=auto eol=lf`, the mechanism this repo
  already uses for `*.sh` — which would give the Windows checkout an LF working
  tree and let prettier keep its enforcing default for every tool, not just
  this one. It is not taken here because it rewrites every file in the owner's
  working tree on the next checkout, and because the risk `auto` leaves is
  currently empty: measured at HEAD, 0 of 186 checkable blobs are CRLF-only and
  0 are mixed. If one ever is, `auto` will hide it on Windows and fail on the
  runner — that is the day to add the attribute.

## D85

**`Річна` keeps ONE `daysHeld` span shared by every asset — it is not computed
per asset. D5#5 stands in that sense, and O23 closes without moving a figure.**
Owner's ruling, 2026-08-24.

**The wording matters, because D80 landed the same day and moved the other half.**
D5#5 originally pinned a PORTFOLIO-WIDE basis; **D80 superseded that** — under a
window the span is the WINDOW's, not the portfolio's. What O23 asked, and what
survives, is the PER-ASSET question: whichever span is in force, every row is
divided by the same one. An earlier draft of this entry said "portfolio-wide"
flat and put two adjacent rows in the decisions index saying opposite things.

The question had been open since 2026-08-18 with its evidence computed on the
19th: should each asset be annualized over its own holding period rather than
the portfolio's span?

**IT WAS ALWAYS A QUESTION ABOUT ONE ROW.** REIT and Energy were bought on the
portfolio's first day, so their own basis IS the global one; …8976 moves by
**0,072 pp** (global 6,2195 %, own-basis 6,2918 %) — the 0,1 quoted in the
evidence table is a difference of two already-rounded figures. Everything rests on …6475, bought 02.06.2026 — 55 days against 174 —
and it rests on it only because that position is young.

**THE DECIDING NUMBER IS NOT THE ANNUALIZED COLUMN.** It is `проти очікуваної`,
which is what the screen asks the reader to judge. Per-asset, …6475 reads as
beating its own expected yield by **+19,3 pp** — on a bond whose coupon is
contractually fixed at 15,2 %. A fixed-coupon instrument cannot outperform its
own contract by nineteen points. That figure is not a measurement of the bond;
it is 55 days of accrual multiplied by 6,6.

**THE PER-ASSET ANSWER ALREADY EXISTS AND ALREADY DISCLAIMS ITSELF.** `XIRR` is
money-weighted over each asset's own flows and dates — it IS the per-asset
column, it reads +99,4 % for …6475, and its header carries `(річн.)` precisely
because a short history annualizes badly. **That mark measures the WINDOW, not
the asset** — `xirrIsExtrapolatedIn` tests the window's own length — so between
2027-02-03 and 2027-06-02 the header will read plain `XIRR` while …6475 is still
annualized from under a year of its own history. The disclaimer is real and is
not per-asset; it is cited here for what it shows about intent, not as a
guarantee. Making `Річна` agree with it would
leave the table with two extrapolations and no comparable figure, and
comparability between rows is the first thing a reader of a table uses.

**WHAT CHANGED SINCE THE EVIDENCE WAS WRITTEN, and what made this closable.**
The strongest argument for switching was that the global basis lies *silently*.
It no longer does. **D80** (2026-08-24) greys `Річна` and `проти очікуваної` for
any row whose holding falls more than 10 % short of its basis — which on this
portfolio is exactly …6475, the one row the whole question was about — and the
footnote names the basis date in every window. The distortion is now disclosed
at the point of reading. **The honest column and the comparable column stopped
being alternatives.**

**THIS ALSO SETTLES THE TWO THINGS THE EVIDENCE LEFT OPEN** (its § 6):

- *Labelling.* No new copy. The basis is stated twice already — the footnote
  names the date it is measured from, and D80's legend explains the grey.
- *`/attributes`' `ФАКТИЧНА (РІЧНА)` card.* It keeps the global basis too. The
  "comparable between rows" argument really is weaker on a card read one asset
  at a time — but **A43**'s review (D80's branch) had already forced the opposite
  point: the same figure must not be asserted differently on two screens, which is why that card
  now carries D80's mark as well. Splitting the basis would reintroduce exactly
  the divergence that mark exists to prevent. **On today's data the mark fires on
  nothing there** — the fact lives in the non-coupon branch, both OVDPs take the
  bond branch, so …6475's figure is not on that screen at all. The guard is for
  the first market asset bought mid-basis, not for the seed.

**Nothing in `src/` changes BEHAVIOUR** — two comments do, and nothing else. `derive.test.ts` keeps asserting +10,9 % with the
words "NOT per-asset basis", `navigation-map.md` keeps "NOT +34,5 %", and every
D5-pinned figure stands. The v1 simplification is re-affirmed on measurement
rather than inherited by default — which is what an open question is for.

*(**The distortion never disappears, and an earlier draft of this entry said it
does.** The two bases are 119 days apart and stay 119 days apart forever — only
the RATIO decays. Measured through the shipped `basisIsShort`: on 2026-11-23
…6475 is 174 own days against 293 global, still 40,6 % short and still greyed;
on 2027-06-02, 365 against 484, still 24,6 % short and still greyed; the
shortfall only crosses the 10 % tolerance at 1 190 global days — **May 2029**.
The draft compared a future own-basis against today's frozen 174, which is what
made it read as convergence. What is true is the weaker claim: the distortion is
worst when a position is new, and D80's grey covers it for as long as it
matters — which is years, not months.)*

## D86

**SMIDA's open-data API is not fetched by this project's code. Categorically,
and not pending an answer — O25 closes without the email it was filed against.**
Owner's ruling, 2026-08-24.

**THE RESEARCH IS THE POINT OF THIS ENTRY.** O25 was filed as "answerable by one
email", which made it look like a question waiting on someone else. It was not.
Everything needed to decide it was measurable, and the measurement contradicted
the assumption the question rested on.

### What O25 assumed, and what the archive actually shows

O25 supposed the blanket `Disallow` "was written for the HTML site and never
revisited when the API shipped" — an assumption about intent, and the reason an
email seemed necessary. **The archive contradicts it, but not in the way a first
pass of this entry claimed.** Measured from the Wayback CDX index by digest
transition:

| period | `smida.gov.ua/robots.txt` |
|---|---|
| → 2020-06-30 | `200` — `User-agent: *` / `Disallow: /` |
| **2020-07-01 → 2021-11-06** | **`404` — no file at all, for sixteen months** |
| *2021-09-27* | *first Wayback capture of `/db/api/v1/` — **inside that gap*** |
| 2021-11-16 → 2024-06-30 | `200` — `Disallow: /db/` · `Allow: /db/prof/` · `Crawl-delay: 1` |
| 2024-07-01 → today | `200` — `Disallow: /` plus per-engine allowances |

**Two corrections to an earlier draft of this entry, both found in review.** It
said the targeted rule began 2022-03 (it began **2021-11-16**) and that "`/db/`
has never been open to a generic agent" — **which is false.** For sixteen months
there was no `robots.txt`, and RFC 9309 §2.3.1.3 is explicit: *"If a server
status code indicates that the robots.txt file is unavailable to the crawler,
then the crawler MAY access any resources on the server."* The API shipped into
exactly that window.

**What the corrected record supports is a stronger claim, not a weaker one.**
The file did not merely get revisited — it was **absent when the API launched and
came back seven weeks later carrying a rule about `/db/`**, the API's own parent
path. So the `/db/` rule POSTDATES the API rather than predating it. O25's
premise is wrong in the opposite direction from the one it feared, and the
question of intent is answered by the record: whoever restored that file in
November 2021 wrote `/db/` into it while the API was already live.

### The three sources, read exactly

- **The statute licenses the USE.** Art. 10¹ of Law 2939-VI, verbatim: public
  information in open-data form is information «у форматі, що дозволяє її
  **автоматизоване оброблення** електронними засобами»; it «**є дозволеною для її
  подальшого вільного використання та поширення**»; and it may be used, including
  commercially, «**з обов'язковим посиланням на джерело отримання такої
  інформації**». SMIDA restates the condition: "гіперпосилання на
  https://smida.gov.ua обов'язкове".
- **And the closing phrase cuts against the distinction this entry leans on.**
  «джерело **отримання**» — the source of OBTAINING — is retrieval-flavoured
  wording, so the use/retrieval line is not as clean as a first draft of this
  entry drew it. It still holds that a licence to reuse is not a right to take by
  any means from any door; it no longer holds that the statute says nothing about
  obtaining.
- **RFC 9309's famous line is NOT where this entry first put it.** "These rules
  are not a form of access authorization" is the last sentence of **§1
  Introduction** (line 100), immediately after "…rules that crawlers are
  requested to honor when accessing URIs" — addressed to crawler behaviour, not,
  as a first draft claimed, to operators. Read exactly, it says robots.txt **is
  not an access-control mechanism**. That supports neither side: it does not make
  ignoring the file permitted, and it does not make honouring it compulsory. What
  decides is §2.3.1.3 for the gap years and the plain current file for today.

### What was checked and found not to help

- **No alternative licensed route to THIS feed.** Publisher-scoped,
  `data.gov.ua` carries **19** НКЦПФР datasets (an earlier free-text count said
  13), all CC-BY — 81 resources on data.gov.ua's own storage and 11 on
  `stockmarket.gov.ua`. The ЗІБД ones resolve to the latter, the archive frozen
  at 2019–2021 (§2 of MARKET-DATA-SOURCES). The other datasets are live but are
  registers and reports — **none carries NAV or a price series**, which is why
  they substitute for nothing here. **SMIDA/АРІФРУ is not a publisher there at
  all** (`organization_autocomplete` returns nothing for it).
- **No commercial product is undercut.** SMIDA's published services are
  issuer-side — disclosure publication, compliance, negotiated extractions. API
  access is not among them.
- **No terms forbid automated access.** No terms-of-use page was found; the only
  stated condition anywhere is the attribution hyperlink. The `Disallow` carries
  this on its own.

### The benefits, stated in full — because the decision is not "it is worthless"

An earlier framing called the feed "regulatory metadata nobody asked for". That
undersold it, and a decision that undersells its own cost is one somebody
reopens. What it would actually give:

1. **Primary-source fund filings, which is where NAV lives.** The index carries
   no prices — but each `<item>`'s `href` points at `report.xml`, and an ІСІ
   periodic report states вартість чистих активів and unit counts.
2. **Corporate actions filterable by holding** (`?edrpou=`), at 9 minutes'
   freshness: rule changes, liquidation, auditor reports, КУА changes.
3. **The only live first-party Ukrainian source found.** Everything else is
   frozen, mirrors the frozen one, or is voluntary commercial disclosure.
4. **The best provenance available** — two qualified e-signatures per item.
5. **Near-free change detection** — `limit=0` returns `time_max` with no body.

### Why they do not carry

- **The part that matters already has a permitted road.** D83 cleared automated
  fetching of the provider's price files through the allowed offer page. W15 is
  served without this.
- **(1) and (2) are a project, not a fetch.** The index yields a `href`; turning
  that into a series means parsing ІСІ report XML at quarterly cadence — and the
  app needs a daily series, which quarterly filings cannot be.
- **(2) is a capability the app does not have at all.** Buying a permanent rule
  exception to enable a feature that does not exist is buying the wrong thing
  first.
- **And the cost is a rule, not a request.** Rule 6 would have to become "a
  named `Disallow` is final EXCEPT for documented APIs" — inherited by every
  future source, with no bright line, and applied by sessions with less context.
  It would also invert the rule's own order: stricter on `pfts.org.ua`, which
  says nothing, than on SMIDA, which says no.

### Why "categorically" rather than "for now"

A provisional "no" invites re-litigation by anyone who reads the statute and not
the archive — which is precisely how this question was born. **Reading by hand is
not what this governs; our code not fetching it is.** If SMIDA ever carves the
API path out of `robots.txt`, or if a document-parsing project is actually
scheduled and something is at stake, that is a NEW decision with new facts — not
a resumption of this one. At that point the email is worth writing, because
there would be something to spend it on.

**Where the measuring stopped.** `MARKET-DATA-SOURCES.md` §1 records the feed's
`size` advancing over ~30 minutes, "verified by curl and by browser" — repeated
automated GETs of the very path this entry rules off-limits, taken on 2026-08-24
while the live file already read `Disallow: /`. **That measurement predates the
ruling and is not repeated.** It is left in the reference because a decision
should show its evidence; it is named here because an entry whose defence is
"our code does not fetch it" cannot quietly rest on our code having fetched it.

*(Sources measured 2026-08-24: `smida.gov.ua/robots.txt` and its Wayback
history, `smida.gov.ua/db/api/v1`, `data.gov.ua` CKAN `package_search` (free-text and
publisher-scoped) and `organization_autocomplete`, `smida.gov.ua/services`,
RFC 9309 §1 / §2.3.1.3 / §3, and art. 10¹ of Law 2939-VI as published on
`zakon.rada.gov.ua`. No terms-of-use page exists to cite.)*

## D87

**The number grammar FOLLOWS THE LANGUAGE, and every editable field groups as
its own parser reads. O26 closes.** Owner's ruling, 2026-08-25.

| | grouping | decimal |
|---|---|---|
| **uk** | any whitespace | **`,` and `.` both** |
| **en** | any whitespace, and `,` when it groups threes | `.` |

**Unchanged in both:** when BOTH marks appear, the last one is the decimal and
the other is grouping, so `1,234.56` and `1.234,56` still both read as 1234.56.
The language rule governs only a LONE mark.

**And a lone mark is where this costs something, so it is stated here rather
than found later.** With `GROUPED_INTEGER` restricted to English, a Ukrainian
field takes a pasted `10,000` as **10** and `1,234` as **1.234**, silently —
both pass `.positive()`. The app's own displays are mostly safe from this
because money always carries two decimals and therefore both marks; the exposed
cases are `numWhole` (the English sidebar's `₴149,016`) and any text from
outside the app. The ruling accepts that: a person typing into their own tracker
does not group by hand. **A refinement exists if the cost ever bites** — a lone
comma with MORE THAN ONE three-digit group (`1,234,567`) is unambiguous even in
Ukrainian, since no number carries two decimal marks — and it is deliberately
NOT taken here, because it is a change to the ruling and not a detail of it.

**What was wrong.** In Ukrainian a typed `6,164` was read as **6164**, so a
percent field rejected it against `.max(100)`. The cause is one rule —
`GROUPED_INTEGER`, `/^[+-]?\d{1,3}(,\d{3})+$/` — which exists for a real
reason: English formats 6164 units as `6,164`, and reading that comma as a
decimal point turned 6164 units into 6.164 on an untouched Save. A36 made the
DISPLAY side safe by having `f.input` parse its own output back, but a user
still could not type the natural Ukrainian form. **So the rule is not deleted —
it becomes English-only**, which is the whole of the parser change.

**The keyboard layout is not the signal, and cannot be.** The ruling first asked
for the user's active layout. `navigator.keyboard.getLayoutMap()` is Chromium
only, requires a secure context, and reports *physical key → character* — it
says nothing about whether a person writes `6,164` or `6.164`; Firefox and
Safari expose nothing at all. The signal that does exist is the app's own
language setting, which has been there since D54/D58 and is already what
`makeFormat` binds.

**Why per language rather than one grammar for both.** A single grammar was on
the table and is cheaper — three lines, and D58's one-parser property intact.
It was rejected because **what a field SHOWS must be what its parser READS**:
Contract 0 already makes display follow the language, so one grammar would have
forced English fields to group with spaces and put an input on screen that
disagreed with every other number around it.

**Accepting `.` in the Ukrainian field costs nothing and buys the numeric
keypad**, which emits a dot under most layouts. It is unambiguous only because
uk grouping is a space: with no comma-as-grouping case in that language, a lone
`.` has exactly one meaning.

**Every editable numeric field groups LIVE, as it is typed** — `1 256 589` in
uk, `1,256,589` in en — through one shared component rather than per call site.
While TYPING, the digits are the value and the grouping marks are the field's
own to insert and move.

**A PASTE is not typing, and the difference is load-bearing.** Pasted text goes
through the full grammar — the both-marks rule included — BEFORE the field
re-prints it. A field that merely stripped every mark and re-grouped the digits
would turn a pasted `1,234.56` into `123 456`, a 100 000x error on the exact
input the rule above calls safe.

**Every in-flight numeric STRING is re-formatted when the language changes —
not only the quote draft.** On the switch each is parsed with the old language
and printed with the new through `f.input`; a string mid-typing (`6,`) does not
parse and is left exactly as it is.

**Naming only `useDraft` here would have missed the field that produced O26.**
`src/state/draft.ts` holds the daily quotes and nothing else. The rest live in
react-hook-form state (`AssetForm`, `TransactionPanel`) and in `useState` —
Settings' USD rate through a LAZY INITIALISER that never re-runs, and
`/allocation`'s target drafts. The concrete failure: in Ukrainian a user types
`6,164` into `expectedPct` (6.164), switches to English, and Saves without
touching it — English reads `6,164` as 6164, and `expectedPct` is
`quoteInputSchema`, which has **no upper bound**. 6164 % is stored. That is the
same silent 1000x that started O26, in the same field.

**The quote draft carries one extra constraint: the reformat must PRESERVE
provenance.** `setQuote` deliberately deletes `origins[assetId]` — typing claims
the row for G5, which is what makes the S2 chip say `manual`. Rewriting a
machine-filled draft through it would relabel every fetched row as the user's
and make the next `reconcileFetched` treat it as user-owned. The reformat goes
through `fillQuote` or its own action, never `setQuote`.

**What this supersedes.** D58 kept ONE locale-blind parser for both languages on
purpose; **that half of it is replaced here.** The rest of D58 stands untouched —
one dictionary, and the language owning its format.

**What it costs, stated rather than discovered later.**
`normalizeNumberInput` is called today with no language, and both
`quoteInputSchema` and `percentInputSchema` are STATIC module-level exports
nested inside other schemas. **The full reach, because a partial list is worse
than none:** `assetFormSchema` (`units`, `expectedPct`, `targetPct`),
`transactionSchema` (`amount` — a different schema, not the asset form), and
`optionalAmount` → `couponAmount`. Making the grammar language-aware reaches all
of them, and **this decision does not choose how** — a factory per language, or
normalising at the component boundary before zod sees the string, are both open.

**Not every string reaching the parser was typed by a human.** Machine fills
write drafts through `f.num` (`useQuoteFetch`, and `DailyQuotes`' prefill and
copy-yesterday paths), and `QuoteRow` and `DailyQuotes` then parse those same
strings back. It works under both grammars — `f.num` output carries either a
space or both marks — but the claim "every consumer is user-typed" would have
been false, and a language-aware parser has to be right for the machine's
strings too. `f.input`'s self-verification is the third consumer, and there the
language is already bound.

**One named field does NOT use this parser at all:** Settings' lead days goes
through `parseLeadDays`, which is `/^\d+$/` and rejects any grouping mark. A
shared field that emits one would break it, so either lead days keeps a plain
input or `parseLeadDays` moves with it. A46 decides which; it may not be
overlooked.

**And one thing it deletes.** `f.input`'s trailing-zero disambiguation —
`6,1640` for 6.164 — exists only because a lone Ukrainian comma could be read as
grouping. With that gone the guarantee stays but the workaround should not.

Implementation is `PLAN-NOW.md` A46.

## D89

**The backup vault takes a GOVERNANCE lock whose floor is read from the live
plan, not a compliance one (2026-08-25)**

Found by a routine sweep of the whole AWS estate, which came back green on every
other axis: stack `IN_SYNC` on 19 of 20 resources and the twentieth a log group
CloudFormation does not evaluate, six alarms `OK`, all 14 nightly backups since
2026-08-12 `COMPLETED`, DLQ empty, cost ≈ $0. The vault itself read
`Locked: false` — so a single `delete-recovery-point` was the entire distance
between the archive and nothing.

**This is the seventh instance of the defect the index names**, after the six
listed under *A pattern these entries kept finding* — D43, D44, D48, D49, D50,
D53. The vault's 15 recovery points looked safe because deletion protection on
the *cluster* is a different control, guarding a different verb, that nothing had
been attempted against. Green because nothing was tried is the recurring shape,
and it has now been found once in roughly every ten decisions.

**Governance, and the reason is reversibility.** Compliance mode cannot be lifted
after its cooling-off period — not by the owner, not by AWS. The archive's schema
is still moving: W3/W4 close 2026-09-02 and a cluster rebuild is on the table. A
lock that outlives every reason it was taken is a liability, not a control. The
Cohasset/SEC 17a-4 case that compliance mode exists for is not this project's.

**`min` and no `max`, and that is one number rather than two.** AWS accepts a job
whose retention is *equal to or longer than* the floor, so the real invariant is
`min <= DeleteAfterDays` — not equality, which is the form that looks tidier and
is the dangerous one. Setting `max` as well would forbid retention being
*lengthened*, which protects nothing and would add a second number to keep in
step with the plan forever. What is worth forbidding is retention being weakened;
that is exactly `min`.

**And the floor is READ FROM THE LIVE PLAN rather than written as a literal**,
which is the part that took a review to see. The bootstrap script only ever
*creates* a plan; on a re-run it leaves an existing one alone. So a literal floor
plus an edited `DeleteAfterDays` would raise the vault above a plan that never
moved, and AWS does not warn about that — it rejects every nightly job, in
silence, until `BackupAgeHours` crosses 48 two nights later. The first draft of
this decision handled that with a comment asking two numbers to be edited
together. A comment is not an enforcement mechanism for a failure whose signature
is a total backup outage nobody is told about.

**And deriving it closes the route, not the state** — a distinction worth keeping
because the first draft of this paragraph claimed the stronger thing. Editing the
plan directly to shorten retention below a standing floor still reaches the bad
state, and nothing re-derives afterwards. That move is exactly what a cluster
rebuild invites, so the rule is: **lower the floor first.**

**Stated as what it is, not as more than it is.** The lock does not stop this
account's human admin, who holds `backup:DeleteBackupVaultLockConfiguration` and
can lift it. What it does is turn a one-command accident into a deliberate
two-step act, and stop every principal that lacks that permission — which today
is every role in the account except the human one. Claiming immutability here
would be the same overclaim as calling an unrestored backup a backup.

**And it was proved by attempting it, not by reading the flag** — the same
standard D48 applied when it restored a backup instead of trusting one. A lock
nobody has tried is exactly the "green because nothing was attempted" shape this
decision opens by naming. Of the 15 points, 14 name the plan in `CreatedBy` and
exactly one is on-demand — 2026-08-18 12:47, `CreatedBy: null` — and it happens
to match that day's scheduled point byte for byte (36 635 253 B). That made it
the only safe target in the vault: refusal costs nothing, and success would have
cost a duplicate of a day that stays covered. Deleting it as admin returned
`InvalidRequestException: RecoveryPoint cannot be deleted or updated (Backup
vault configured with Lock)`, exit 254, count still 15, target still
`COMPLETED`.

**Why that on-demand point exists is not recorded, and this entry does not
guess** — it is not the durability-test backup, which was 2026-08-11 at 34.6 MiB
and is gone. It matters only because it carries no lifecycle at all where every
scheduled point carries `DeleteAfterDays: 35`, so it never expires.

**The lock freezes lifecycles too, and that is the consequence most likely to
bite.** AWS: Vault Lock "prevents attempts to update the lifecycle policy that
controls the retention period of any recovery point currently stored in a backup
vault." The narrower and more comforting fact — that points saved before the lock
are not re-measured against the floor — is about *enforcement*, not mutability,
and the two are easy to conflate. In the cluster-rebuild scenario named three
paragraphs up, draining the vault by shortening retention is the obvious move,
and it now requires lifting the lock first.

**The trap, recorded because it has no error message.** Governance and compliance
are not a mode argument — they are the *presence* of `--changeable-for-days`.
Omitting it is governance; passing it, minimum 3, starts the countdown to
permanence. The wrong choice looks like a harmless extra flag, and it is
irreversible by construction.

Applied by `infra/scripts/bootstrap-backups.sh`, which is where the vault, role,
plan and selection already live — deliberately outside `template.yaml`, for the
reason recorded there. The script now ends by *reading back* the lock state
rather than announcing it, for the reason this entry opens with: a line printing
"lock applied" on any 2xx is the same green-because-nothing-was-read defect, one
layer down. `LockDate` is the tell — null is governance, a date is compliance.
Implementation is `PLAN-NOW.md` A49.

## D91

**One aliased column cost ~64 DPU a night: `ORDER BY as_of` bound to the query's
own `to_char()` output, not to the indexed column (2026-08-25)**

D90 parked a question against D48 and named W6 as its decider. Four drafts of
this entry answered it from correlation and every one was wrong. It is answered
here from `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, VERBOSE)` against the live cluster, which prints a
**`Statement DPU Estimate`** block — per-statement cost, on demand, and the tool
none of the guessing needed to happen without.

**Four measurements.** All on `price_capture`, 6,664 rows, ~35 MiB stored.

| Statement | Plan | Read DPU | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| streak query, `source='inzhur'`, **verbatim** | Index Scan | **0.559** | 4.1 ms |
| streak query, `source='nbu_fv'`, **verbatim** | **Full Scan (btree-table)** | **64.989** | 826.5 ms |
| same, `ORDER BY price_capture.as_of` (column, not alias) | **Index Scan Backward** | **9.508** | 152.2 ms |
| `count(*)` — projects nothing | Index Only Scan, all 6,664 rows | **1.210** | 26.9 ms |

**The cause is an alias collision, and row three is the controlled test.** The
query selects `to_char(as_of, 'YYYY-MM-DD') AS as_of` and then orders by
`as_of`. In PostgreSQL a bare name in `ORDER BY` resolves to the *output* column
first, so the sort key is the TEXT expression — visible in the plan as
`Sort Key: (to_char(...)) DESC`. A sort on a computed value cannot inherit index
order, so `LIMIT 60` bounds nothing and every matching row must be materialised
first. Changing only that clause to name the column flips the plan to
`Index Scan Backward` and drops the read **6.8×**.

**Why it emptied the wallet is the storage layout, and row four is that test.**
`count(*)` walks all 6,664 rows through the index for **1.210 DPU**; the scanning
branch walks `B-Tree Scan on public.price_capture` — the table, whose rows carry
`payload_gzip`, 32 MB compressed. Scanning the index is cheap; scanning the table
is not. Full working, plans and the guard's own figures:
`infra/README.md` § "Re-measured 2026-08-25".

**What that does to D48 is narrower than either "confirmed" or "refuted".** D48
says *"DSQL projects only the columns the query asks for. `payload_gzip` is never
read."* Measured: true of index paths — 6,664 rows for 1.210 DPU — and false of a
table scan, where the payload is billed whatever the projection says.
`Projections:` names what comes back, not what was read. **A2's rejection of the
payload split therefore stands for the queries it was measured on, and is not a
general licence**: any plan that reaches `B-Tree Scan on public.price_capture`
pays for the payload.

**And D48's index was right all along.** `capture.ts` recorded the design intent —
*"`requested_at` is the third key so the streak query's `ORDER BY` is served by
the same index... the planner can walk an ascending index backwards"* — and row
three proves the index can do exactly that. It was never given the chance,
because the query's own alias hid the column being ordered on. **The index was
capable and the SQL prevented its use**, which no latency measurement on the
21-row `inzhur` branch could have exposed.

**What is still not known.** Why row three costs 9.508 rather than ~1: its plan
shows `Storage Lookup ... Filters: ok AND quotes_sha256 IS NOT NULL, Rows Removed
by Filter: 360`, so the two predicates outside the index force heap fetches — a
reading of the plan, not a measurement. Nothing here tested it.

**Cost now that A20 has removed the call entirely:** **~173 DPU/month, 0.17%** of
the 100,000 tier, against ~325 projected for year 1 at a much larger archive —
the two are not comparable and the projection is not refuted.

**D90's guard correction stands** (~73 DPU/month against D64's ~6). Its
allowance-share half is superseded: 1.6% described the seven days this query was
scanning.

**The pattern is still deployed, and that is the actionable half.** `observeNbu`
runs on every successful capture with the same shape — `to_char(as_of, …) AS
as_of` … `ORDER BY as_of` — and it selects `payload_gzip`. Measured: **0.356 DPU**
over its production 7-day `BETWEEN` window (Index Scan on `price_capture_as_of`),
and **64.979 DPU** with the range opened, which is what a manual `{observe:{}}`
does. Only the window stands between it and the same scan. `PLAN-NOW.md` **A50**
carries the audit.

**A third correction to D48 falls out of that.** D48 calls the old
`(as_of, requested_at)` index "dead weight — no query leads with it".
`observeNbu`'s `WHERE as_of BETWEEN` does lead with it, and it is the only thing
bounding that query. **Dropping it as dead weight turns 0.356 DPU into 64.979,
nightly.**

**Three lessons, and the middle one is the expensive one.**

1. **`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, VERBOSE)` prints per-statement DPU.** Four rounds of
   inference from what disappeared out of a diff, all wrong, before anyone ran it.
2. **A query that runs per source is not verified until every branch is planned.**
   D48 recorded one `735 ms → 2.26 ms` figure; the branch it did not plan matched
   58% of the table instead of 0.3% and scanned nightly for a week.
3. **An output alias that shadows a column silently disables an index.** It
   type-checks, it returns correct rows, and it changes the plan.
