## D71 — `as_of` is per source, and the Inzhur rows were a day early — supersedes the convention pinned in `001_price_capture.sql`

**2026-08-18.** Found by W1, confirmed twice, and repaired the same day.

**`as_of` for `inzhur` is the Kyiv date of the RUN. For `nbu_fv` it stays the
Kyiv date minus one.** One function served both until now, and the premise it
rested on — *"the feed refreshes ~13:00, so the 01:00 run reads the price settled
the previous day"* — is false for one source and true for the other, so it had to
be wrong for one of them.

**MEASURED, AND THEN MEASURED AGAIN A DIFFERENT WAY.** Inverting the DCF over the
published coupon schedule dates a quote from the schedule alone, independently of
anything the provider says about its own calendar:

| DCF says the price is for | value | our archive filed it as |
|---|---|---|
| 08-15 | 1065.2373 | **08-14** (1065.24) |
| 08-16 | 1065.6592 | **08-15** (1065.66) |
| 08-17 | 1066.0812 | **08-16** (1066.08) |
| **08-18** | **1066.5035** | **08-17** (1066.50) |

Four consecutive days, a one-day offset every time, residual **0.0035 ₴** —
inside the 0.0007–0.0046 band D31 recorded for a fresh, correctly-dated bond.
Independently: Inzhur answered that fund prices are flat **Saturday–Monday**,
while our archive showed the plateau on **Friday–Sunday**. Shift ours one day and
the two statements are identical. **The provider was right and our labels were
not** — which is the reverse of what the W1 note first concluded, and the
correction is kept in that note rather than tidied away.

**Why NBU is untouched, and this is the part worth reading twice.** For NBU the
same value is not a label at all — it is the **request parameter**.
`nbuFairValueUrl(asOf)` asks for a named date's file, so the date requested and
the date labelled are the same by construction. **6 636 rows back to 2016-01-04
were never affected**, and changing the shared function outright would have
broken every one of them. The fix is to split the meanings, not to move the
subtraction: `inzhurAsOf` and `nbuAsOf`, two names, because the conflation is
invisible while they share one.

**The repair.** 14 rows — `source='inzhur'`, 2026-08-10 through 2026-08-17 — all
`as_of + 1`, in one transaction, far under DSQL's 3 000-mutated-row cap. A fresh
recovery point was taken first and confirmed complete, because DSQL has no PITR
(recovery is whole-cluster only, see `infra/README.md`) and the backup is
therefore the only undo. `price_observation` holds 418 rows and **every one is
`nbu_fv`** — the Inzhur half is still W3/W4 — so nothing downstream carried the
bad dates.

**Done before W4 on purpose.** W4 designs the Inzhur observation schema, whose
natural key is `(as_of, ref, basis, source)`. DSQL primary keys are immutable, so
a wrong date is survivable in a journal table and is a DROP/CREATE in a key. The
cost of waiting was not the 14 rows; it was that the number grows daily and that
the deadline was structural.

**What it cost to find.** Nothing detected this. Every alarm was green, every row
was written, and the payloads were correct throughout — only the label was wrong.
It surfaced because a support answer disagreed with the archive and the
disagreement was chased instead of dismissed. That is the reusable part: **the
provider contradicting your data is evidence about your data.**
