## D121 — The schedule fills itself (2026-08-31)

[`D120`](D120.md) recorded two findings and left them for a ruling. This acts on
both, and they turned out to share one source: the provider's `paymentSchedule`.

## What the feed already knew

The picker has been rendering "matures 24.03.2027" as a hint since P3 and writing it
**nowhere**. Three hand-typed fields were sitting in that same payload:

| field | read from |
|---|---|
| `maturity` | `assetDetails.maturityDate`, verbatim |
| `nextCoupon` | `nextPaymentOnOrAfter(schedule, today)` |
| `payoutSchedule` | the median gap between distinct payment dates |
| `couponRatePct` | the RECURRING coupon × payments-per-year ÷ ₴1000 |

**FOUR, not three** — the rate joined during review. It comes from the same
schedule, [`D119`](D119.md) gives the derivation exactly, and it is the field most
expensive to get wrong: a typo mis-scales every coupon figure the asset produces
with nothing to cross-check against. It is read from the coupon that **repeats**,
not the smallest payment — a bond issued mid-period pays a short stub first, and
taking that as the rate halves everything. Where nothing repeats, no rate is
written. And it is not written at all onto a bond whose ledger cannot be counted
but which carries a legacy `couponAmount`: a rate that cannot answer would
suppress the only figure that asset has.

This is the same move [`D119`](D119.md) made for the coupon rate, and the same
reason: a fact about the instrument should come from the instrument.

**These are not decorative fields.** D120 measured how load-bearing they are —
`nextCoupon` anchors the coupon grid, starts the unsettled walk and dates the payout
projection; `maturity` stops the ghost accrual, clamps the roll and raises the
maturity reminder. Filling them right matters more than filling them at all.

## The cadence is read in BANDS

```
< 45 days   → monthly
< 135       → quarterly
< 250       → semiannual
otherwise   → nothing
```

**Not equality on 182.** The measured norm is 182 days — 26 weeks, which is why a
schedule keeps its weekday for life — but `UA4000235782` carries a **183 then 181**
pair around a payment moved by a day
([`OVDP-COUPON-STRUCTURE.md`](../reference/OVDP-COUPON-STRUCTURE.md)). Exact
matching would misread that bond; the bands read it correctly, and the **median**
gap means one shifted date cannot move the answer at all.

**One payment date means `maturity`** — a zero-coupon bond pays once, at the end.

**Outside the bands, nothing is written.** An annual bond has no member in this
enum and an irregular schedule has no cadence; `payoutSchedule` is the divisor in
`couponPerPayment`, so a guess would put a wrong number into every coupon figure the
asset produces. Silence leaves the field to the user, which is the honest failure.

## Overwriting is correct here

A quote fetch may never overwrite a number the user typed (G5). This may, and the
difference is what the value MEANS: a fetched quote is a competing opinion about a
price, while a maturity is a property of the instrument the user has just named. A
date left over from the previously picked bond is not a value worth protecting — it
is exactly the stale-ref problem [`D116`](D116.md) solved one field over by clearing
the ref when the kind changes.

Keyed on the last applied ref, so it fires **once per instrument** rather than every
render, and clearing the ref re-arms it so re-picking the same bond fills again.

## The default pairing was wrong

`assetFormDefaults` opened a new asset as `fixed_coupon` + **`maturity`** — a
zero-coupon bond — while all 32 live bonds and both seed bonds are `semiannual`.
Corrected. Linking overwrites it from the feed anyway; this is what an **unlinked**
bond now opens on, and it is the divisor in the one place a default is most likely
to survive unread.

## What was NOT done

`payoutSchedule` is still **not bound** to `yieldType`, and D120's reasoning stands:
the enum's `maturity` is a real instrument, and funds publish no schedule at all, so
there is nothing to bind for them. A derived default is the whole of the fix.
